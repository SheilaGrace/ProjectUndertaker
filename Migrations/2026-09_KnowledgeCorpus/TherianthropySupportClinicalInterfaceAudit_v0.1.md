# Therianthropy Support and Clinical Interface Audit v0.1

## Purpose

This document audits the boundary between:

Therian identity support,
behavioral safety,
mental-health support,
clinical care,
community mentorship,
external referral,
and public communication.

The purpose is not
to turn community mentors
into clinicians.

The purpose is to identify
which responsibilities can be:

documented,
distributed,
automated,
referred,
or safely left unresolved.


## Core Principles

**Translate, do not automatically normalize.**

**Identity validation does not require behavioral exemption.**

**Behavioral safety does not require identity suppression.**

**Community support is not clinical diagnosis.**

**Mentorship must not become a single point of failure.**

**A mentor should not need to become
a psychiatrist,
therapist,
crisis worker,
behavior specialist,
historian,
translator,
and public-relations representative
in order to support one individual.**


## FIELD PROBLEM: Support Capacity Bottleneck

### Observed Problem

SheilaGrace reports
a persistent mismatch
between the amount of support requested
within Therian community spaces
and the number of people
able to provide sustained,
informed,
and safe support.

The problem was not limited
to answering terminology questions.

Individuals seeking help
could simultaneously require assistance with:

- understanding Therian terminology,
- describing unusual subjective experiences,
- identity exploration,
- behavioral self-regulation,
- interpersonal conflict,
- emotional distress,
- trauma-related difficulties,
- communication with family or institutions,
- navigating mental-health services,
- and explaining Therianthropy
  to people unfamiliar with the subject.

As these needs accumulated,
experienced community members
could become default support points
for multiple unrelated roles.


### Field Evidence / Author Testimony

SheilaGrace describes
Japanese Therian community support
as repeatedly functioning
like a refuge
for distressed or socially injured individuals.

Some individuals arrived
after experiences such as:

- bullying,
- social rejection,
- misunderstanding of non-human identity,
- psychological distress,
- or difficulty obtaining appropriate support elsewhere.

The author also reports
that people who were uncertain
whether they were Therian
sometimes entered the same support spaces
because those spaces appeared
more accepting or safer
than other available environments.

As a result,
a single mentor could be expected
to perform functions resembling:

- terminology guide,
- identity-support peer,
- behavioral-safety advisor,
- emotional supporter,
- conflict mediator,
- clinical translator,
- referral navigator,
- community historian,
- and public-facing representative.

SheilaGrace summarizes
the operational problem as:

"not enough doctors
to handle the number of patients."

This phrase is preserved
as an author metaphor
for support-capacity overload.

It does not imply
that all community members
are patients
or that community mentors
are clinicians.


### Referral Bottleneck Within the Capacity Problem

The author reports
that external referral
did not reliably remove mentor workload.

Mentors could encounter uncertainty about:

- which professional or institution
  was appropriate,
- whether that professional
  understood Therianthropy,
- how to explain Therian identity
  without triggering unnecessary pathologization,
- whether the individual
  would be treated respectfully,
- and what to do
  when previous healthcare experiences
  had already reduced trust.

This means that
"refer to a professional"
was not always
a complete operational solution.

Referral itself
could require translation,
navigation,
risk assessment,
and follow-up support.


### Public Visibility Feedback Loop

SheilaGrace reports
that increased public visibility
appeared to produce
both benefits and additional support demand.

Greater visibility could make it easier
for isolated individuals
to discover terminology
and community.

At the same time,
the author observed
increased arrival of:

- people seeking identity guidance,
- people seeking emotional support,
- curious outsiders,
- people entering during conflict or crisis,
- and individuals whose expectations
  exceeded available mentor capacity.

The author also reports cases
in which unmet expectations,
community conflict,
or dissatisfaction
later contributed
to hostility toward Therian communities.

These observations are preserved
as field testimony.

The frequency,
scale,
and broader representativeness
of these patterns
have not yet been externally established.


### Mentor Saturation Risk

When too many responsibilities
are concentrated in one mentor,
several risks increase:

- exhaustion,
- delayed responses,
- inconsistent support,
- overconfidence outside one's expertise,
- accidental psychological harm,
- conflict escalation,
- dependency on one experienced individual,
- inability to support new arrivals,
- and loss of accumulated community knowledge
  when that mentor withdraws.

The problem therefore cannot be solved
only by asking mentors
to become more knowledgeable.

Increasing mentor competence
without reducing role concentration
may increase,
rather than solve,
the single-point-of-failure problem.


### What Should Not Be Generalized

The current evidence
does not establish that:

- all Therians require mental-health support,
- all Japanese Therian communities
  operate in the same way,
- all people seeking support
  are clinically ill,
- all public visibility
  harms Therian communities,
- all mental-health professionals
  misunderstand Therianthropy,
- or all community mentors
  experience the same workload.

The observations in this section
primarily represent
SheilaGrace's long-term
community-support experience.

External research
and additional community testimony
are still required
before prevalence
or population-level conclusions
are made.


### Design Implications

A sustainable support system
should reduce the number of problems
that automatically escalate
to an experienced mentor.

Possible infrastructure should include:

reference material
→ self-directed description
→ structured identity exploration
→ peer support
→ experienced mentor support
→ specialized professional support
where appropriate.

Individuals should be able
to enter or leave
at different points
depending on their needs.

Reference systems should be designed
to absorb repetitive work such as:

- terminology explanation,
- common experience clarification,
- source navigation,
- preparation for professional consultation,
- and documentation of recurring support questions.

Mentors should remain available
for cases where human judgment,
relationship,
context,
or community knowledge
is genuinely useful.

The system should not assume
that every unresolved problem
must eventually be handled
by one highly experienced individual.


### Project DeepDivers Relevance

This field problem
is a primary design motivation
for Project DeepDivers.

The project should not attempt
to replace all human support.

Its role should include
reducing avoidable mentor load
by preserving:

- terminology,
- provenance,
- examples,
- navigation paths,
- support boundaries,
- referral preparation,
- and recovery paths
  for uncertain or unresolved cases.

A key design goal is:

**Do not make mentorship
a single point of failure.**

## FIELD PROBLEM: Behavioral Safety

### Observed Problem

Therian support
may involve experiences
in which an individual reports:

strong animal-associated urges,

changes in attention,

fear or threat responses,

aggressive impulses,

flight responses,

territorial feelings,

changes in posture or movement,

or other behavior
experienced as related
to a Shift.

Such experiences
do not automatically imply
that the individual is dangerous.

However,
when subjective experience
begins to affect behavior,

support may need
to distinguish:

identity,

internal experience,

impulse,

action,

consequence,

and safety.

The central problem is:

**how to preserve
the individual's identity
without treating
unsafe behavior
as exempt from ordinary consequences.**


### Identity Is Not the Behavior

A Therian identity
should not be judged
by a single behavior.

Likewise,
a behavior should not be justified
solely because
the individual associates it
with Therian identity.

An individual may say:

"I wanted to bite,"

"I felt like running away,"

"I felt territorial,"

"I wanted to growl,"

or

"I felt less human
during that moment."

These statements describe
an internal experience.

They do not by themselves establish:

loss of agency,

clinical disorder,

inevitable action,

or permission
to harm another person.


### Impulse Is Not Action

Project DeepDivers
should distinguish:

an urge

from

an action.

An individual may experience
a strong impulse

without acting on it.

The existence
of an unwanted,
unusual,
or animal-associated impulse

should not automatically
be treated as:

dangerousness,

moral failure,

or psychiatric pathology.

Behavioral safety becomes relevant
when there is:

difficulty controlling action,

risk of injury,

repeated harmful behavior,

major functional disruption,

or credible danger
to the individual
or another person.


### Subjective Experience Does Not Establish Cause

An individual may interpret
a behavior or impulse
as part of a Mental Shift.

That interpretation
should be preserved
as part of
the individual's experience.

However,
the label:

"Mental Shift"

does not by itself establish
the cause
of the behavior.

Possible contributing factors
may include:

ordinary emotion,

stress,

fear,

trauma-related responses,

sleep disruption,

impulsivity,

interpersonal conflict,

environmental pressure,

another psychological
or medical factor,

Therian-related experience,

or multiple interacting factors.

The relationship
may remain uncertain.

Behavioral support
should not require
premature causal certainty.


### Safety Without Identity Suppression

A supporter may say:

"Your experience matters,

and

this action
is not safe."

These statements
are compatible.

The support goal
should not automatically be:

"act more human"

or

"stop being Therian."

The goal should instead be:

reduce harm,

increase agency,

expand safe choices,

and help the individual
express important needs
without unnecessary danger.


### Identity-Congruent Safety Communication

Factually correct guidance
may still fail
if it is expressed
in a framework
the individual cannot
or will not meaningfully use.

A Therian individual
may experience statements such as:

"this is what humans normally do,"

"this is socially expected,"

or

"this is simply
how people should behave"

as disconnected
from their own
self-understanding.

In some cases,
safety information
may become easier to understand

when it is translated
through concepts
that are meaningful
within the individual's
Therian identity.

The purpose
is not to manipulate
the individual.

The purpose is:

**to preserve
the same safety information

while changing
the explanatory frame
through which
that information becomes usable.**


### Identity-Congruent Framing

Where the individual
finds it helpful,
a supporter may use:

species-relevant metaphor,

animal-behavior analogy,

pack or social-group analogy,

territorial or environmental analogy,

caregiving analogy,

or another identity-relevant frame

to discuss:

risk,

consequence,

self-regulation,

conflict avoidance,

care seeking,

or safer alternatives.

For example,
an individual
who experiences themselves
through a canine framework

might find it easier
to consider questions such as:

"What would help you
leave the situation
before you feel cornered?"

"How can you protect
yourself and your group
without injuring anyone?"

or

"What kind of safe place
would allow you
to calm down
before acting?"

Such framing
should remain collaborative.

The supporter should not assume
that a particular species metaphor
will be meaningful
simply because
of the individual's Theriotype.


### Translation Is Not Literalization

Identity-congruent communication
should not convert metaphor
into false factual claims.

A supporter should distinguish:

the individual's
non-human self-understanding

from

the legal,
medical,
and physical conditions
of the material body.

For example,
animal-care language
may sometimes be useful
as metaphor,

but the individual
should not be told
that ordinary veterinary services
are the appropriate treatment system
for a human material body.

Likewise,
legal consequences
should not be replaced
with inaccurate claims
about what happens
to animals.

The principle is:

**translate the meaning,
not the facts.**


### Correct Information Is Not Always Usable Information

A statement may be
technically correct

and still fail
as support.

Safety communication
must consider both:

**accuracy**

and

**uptake.**

Information that cannot be:

understood,

integrated,

remembered,

or applied

may have limited practical value.

This does not justify
changing facts
to make them more acceptable.

It means that
the same factual constraint
may need
more than one explanation.


### Multiple Explanatory Frames

Project DeepDivers
may therefore offer
multiple descriptions
of the same safety issue.

For example:

**Plain practical frame**

What happened,
what risk exists,
and what action
would reduce that risk.

**Identity-congruent frame**

How the same situation
can be understood
using concepts meaningful
to the individual's
Therian self-understanding.

**Clinical-neutral frame**

How the experience
may be described
for a clinician
or other professional.

These frames
should describe
the same underlying situation.

They should not produce
different facts
depending on
which identity framework
is being used.


### Individualization Boundary

A Theriotype
does not automatically determine
how an individual thinks.

For example,
a canine Therian
should not automatically
be assumed to value:

packs,

dominance,

territoriality,

obedience,

predator behavior,

or any other
species stereotype.

Project DeepDivers
should ask:

what the individual's
Theriotype means
to that individual,

which metaphors
they find useful,

and which interpretations
they reject.

**Identity-congruent support
must remain
individual-congruent support.**


### Persuasion and Agency

Identity-congruent framing
should support
informed decision-making,

not bypass it.

The goal is not:

"find the metaphor
that makes the individual obey."

The goal is:

"find a language
in which the individual
can understand the risk
and make a safer decision."

The individual
should remain free
to:

question the explanation,

reject a metaphor,

request another framing,

or propose
a more accurate description
of their own experience.


### Safe Expression

Where possible,
an animal-associated urge
may be redirected
into safer forms of expression.

Possible approaches
may include:

movement,

exercise,

private vocalization
where appropriate,

creative expression,

roleplay,

structured sensory activity,

time in a safe environment,

temporary withdrawal
from conflict,

or another personally meaningful
and non-harmful outlet.

Such alternatives
should not be treated
as universal prescriptions.

Their suitability depends
on:

the individual,

the environment,

physical safety,

age,

consent,

and context.


### Regulation Is Not Suppression

Behavioral self-regulation
should not require
the individual
to deny the existence
of an impulse or identity.

A safer sequence may be:

notice the experience,

describe it,

identify the urge,

separate urge from action,

evaluate consequences,

choose the safest available response,

and review what happened afterward.

The goal is not
to make the experience disappear.

The goal is
to increase the individual's ability
to remain safe
while experiencing it.


### Agency-Preserving Support

Support should preserve
the individual's sense of agency.

Statements such as:

"I could not help it
because I was shifted"

should not automatically
be accepted
as a complete explanation
for harmful behavior.

At the same time,
a supporter should not assume
that every difficult behavior
was fully deliberate.

The relevant questions include:

What did the individual experience?

How much control
did they feel they had?

What actions occurred?

What harm occurred
or nearly occurred?

What factors
made control easier
or harder?

What could reduce risk
next time?

Agency
should be explored,

not simply assumed
or denied.


### Consequences and Accountability

Identity-respecting support
does not remove
ordinary responsibility
for actions.

If another person
is harmed,
frightened,
threatened,
or placed at risk,

that consequence
should be acknowledged.

Accountability may include:

stopping the behavior,

repairing harm
where appropriate,

changing the environment,

setting boundaries,

developing safer responses,

or seeking additional support.

Accountability
should focus on behavior
and consequence,

not on declaring
the identity itself
invalid.


### Change From Baseline

A reported behavior
may deserve additional attention
when it is:

new,

rapidly worsening,

substantially more intense,

more difficult to control,

associated with major sleep change,

associated with confusion,

or significantly different
from the individual's
usual baseline.

The relevant question
is not only:

"Does this happen
during a Shift?"

but also:

**"Is this different
from what is usual
for this individual?"**

Change from baseline
may indicate
that another factor
requires consideration.


### Behavioral Risk Levels

Behavioral safety
should not treat
all unusual behavior
as equivalent.

A future protocol
may distinguish:

**Low immediate risk**

Unusual impulses
or expressions are present,
but the individual
remains able
to choose safe behavior.

**Elevated concern**

Control is becoming difficult,
conflict is increasing,
or behavior
is creating meaningful
social or functional harm.

Additional support
or professional consultation
may be appropriate.

**Immediate safety concern**

There is credible danger
of serious injury,

serious self-harm,

harm toward another person,

major loss of behavioral control,

or another acute emergency.

At this point,
ordinary identity mentoring
should not remain
the primary response.

Crisis Escalation
should be used instead.

These categories
are routing tools.

They are not diagnoses.


### Environmental Design

Behavioral safety
does not depend only
on individual self-control.

Environment may affect:

stress,

sensory load,

social conflict,

available privacy,

ability to withdraw,

physical safety,

and access
to supportive people.

Where possible,
risk reduction
may include changing:

location,

social demands,

sensory environment,

proximity to conflict,

available exit routes,

or timing of difficult interactions.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore consider
both:

individual regulation

and

environmental support.


### Relational Boundary

A supporter may remain:

accepting,

reassuring,

and emotionally present

while also setting
a firm behavioral boundary.

For example:

"I am still here,

but I cannot support
this action."

Relational continuity
should not require
tolerating unsafe behavior.

Likewise,
setting a safety boundary
should not automatically
end relational support.


### Mentor Boundary

A mentor may help with:

identifying triggers,

describing experiences,

planning safer responses,

reviewing consequences,

finding alternative expressions,

and deciding
whether additional help
may be useful.

A mentor should not be required
to become:

a behavior therapist,

psychiatric clinician,

emergency responder,

or permanent supervisor
of another individual's behavior.

When risk exceeds
ordinary peer-support capacity,

the appropriate response
is escalation or referral,

not expansion
of the mentor's role.


### LLM Boundary

An LLM may assist with:

structured reflection,

identifying patterns,

planning safer alternatives,

preparing
for professional consultation,

and helping distinguish:

impulse,

experience,

interpretation,

and action.

An LLM should not claim
that:

a person
will definitely remain safe,

a specific impulse
has a known clinical cause,

a Shift
caused a harmful act,

or professional assessment
is unnecessary.

When credible serious danger
is present,

the system should prioritize
human safety support
and appropriate escalation.


### Reframing the "Animal Trainer" Metaphor

Earlier support material
used animal-training metaphors
to describe
some forms
of behavioral guidance.

Retrospective author clarification
indicates that
this metaphor compressed
two different ideas:

behavioral self-regulation

and

communication adapted
to the individual's
Theriotype-related
frame of reference.

The useful part
should be preserved:

**safety guidance
may become more usable
when translated
into concepts
that are meaningful
to the individual.**

The problematic part
should not be preserved:

the supporter
should not become
a controller
who "trains"
the individual
through authority
or obedience.

Project DeepDivers
therefore replaces
the trainer model
with:

**identity-congruent,
agency-preserving
behavioral translation
and self-regulation.**

The individual
remains
an active participant
in understanding:

their experience,

the meaning
of the explanation,

their available choices,

their environment,

and the consequences
of action.


### What Should Not Be Generalized

The current record
does not establish that:

Mental Shifts
normally cause aggression,

Therians
are more dangerous
than other people,

animal-associated impulses
inevitably become actions,

unusual behavior
is necessarily psychiatric,

all behavioral difficulty
is caused by Therian identity,

or every Shift experience
requires intervention.

Behavioral safety
should respond
to actual risk,

not stereotypes
about Therian identity.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core behavioral-safety principle is:

**Support the identity;
evaluate the behavior separately.**

A second principle is:

**Impulse is not action.**

A third principle is:

**Regulation
does not require
identity suppression.**

A fourth principle is:

**When safety changes,
reassess the situation
rather than assuming
the existing explanation
still fits.**

Project DeepDivers
should help individuals
retain:

identity,

agency,

and dignity

while reducing
avoidable harm.


A fifth principle is:

**Safety guidance
must be accurate in content

and accessible in framing.**

## FIELD PROBLEM: Clinical Translation

### Observed Problem

Therian individuals
and mental-health or medical professionals
may use substantially different frameworks
to describe the same reported experience.

A Therian may describe:

identity,
Shifting,
Species Dysphoria,
phantom body experience,
animal-associated urges,
or spiritual interpretation.

A clinician may instead be listening for:

distress,
functional impairment,
perception,
belief,
risk,
trauma history,
mood,
attention,
sleep,
or other clinically relevant information.

Neither vocabulary
automatically translates into the other.

When translation fails,
the individual may feel
misunderstood or pathologized,

while the clinician
may receive insufficient information
to understand
what actually requires support.


### Translation Is Not Conversion

Clinical translation
should not convert
Therian identity
into psychiatric terminology.

It should also not convert
all psychological distress
into Therian terminology.

The goal is:

**preserve the individual's meaning
while making clinically relevant information legible.**

Translation should therefore retain
separate layers:

the individual's identity,

the reported experience,

the individual's interpretation,

the distress or functional concern,

and the specific reason
professional support is being sought.


### Two-Way Translation Problem

Translation is required
in both directions.

**Community → Clinician**

The professional may need
a concise explanation of:

what Therianthropy means
to this individual,

which terminology is community-specific,

what experiences
are being reported,

and which parts
the individual does or does not
consider problematic.

**Clinician → Individual**

The individual may need help understanding:

what the clinician is asking,

why a question
is clinically relevant,

what a diagnostic term means,

what remains uncertain,

and whether the clinician
is addressing distress
or making a judgment
about identity.

Project DeepDivers
should support both directions.


### Clinical Competence Is Not Identity-Translation Competence

A clinician may correctly understand:

the presenting problem,

relevant symptoms,

functional impairment,

risk,

and an appropriate treatment plan

while still having difficulty
communicating that information
through the individual's
Therian frame of reference.

These are separate competencies.

**Clinical competence**

does not automatically provide

**Therian identity literacy**

or

**identity-congruent
communication competence.**

Likewise,
a Therian mentor
may be highly capable
of identity-congruent communication

without possessing
clinical authority.

Project DeepDivers
should not require
either person
to acquire
the other's entire role.


### Successful Referral Does Not Guarantee Successful Uptake

A referral may be clinically successful

while communication remains incomplete.

For example:

the clinician understands
the actual problem,

the individual receives
appropriate advice,

and no unnecessary
pathologization occurs,

but the explanation
is expressed
in a framework
the individual
does not meaningfully integrate.

The resulting failure
may appear as:

non-adherence,

confusion,

continued distress,

repeated return
to a community mentor,

or the belief
that professional care
"did not understand"
the individual.

Some of these outcomes
may have many causes.

They should not automatically
be attributed
to identity-translation failure.

However,
communication fit
should remain
one possible variable.


### Identity-Congruent Clinical Translation Layer

Project DeepDivers
may provide
a translation layer

between:

clinical information

and

the individual's
identity framework.

The process may be represented as:

clinical assessment
and recommendation

→ factual clinical core

→ identity-congruent explanation

→ individual understanding check

→ clarification or revision
where necessary.

The translation layer
must preserve
the factual clinical core.


### Factual Core Must Remain Stable

Identity-congruent translation
may change:

metaphor,

examples,

word choice,

explanatory frame,

and level of detail.

It should not change:

diagnosis,

medication instructions,

dosage,

medical contraindications,

risk information,

legal requirements,

or the clinician's
actual recommendation.

When uncertainty exists,
the system should return
to the original source

rather than completing
the missing information
through metaphor.

The principle is:

**translate meaning
without mutating
clinical content.**


### Example Architecture

A clinician may communicate:

"This treatment
is intended to reduce
the anxiety response
that is interfering
with sleep."

Project DeepDivers
may preserve that statement
in plain clinical language

and,
if the individual
finds it useful,

also provide
an identity-congruent explanation
such as:

"This is not intended
to remove
your animal identity.

The treatment is aimed
at helping your body
remain out of
a persistent alarm state
so that you can rest."

The second explanation
does not replace
the clinical statement.

It provides
another route
to understanding it.


### Individual Meaning Check

Theriotype alone
is not sufficient
to determine
the correct translation.

A canine Therian
may reject canine metaphors.

Another may find them
highly intuitive.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore ask:

Does this explanation
fit how you understand yourself?

Would a different framing
make more sense?

Did the translation
change anything
the clinician actually said?

The individual
should be able
to correct the model.


### Bidirectional Return Path

Translation should not be
a one-way post-processing step.

If identity-congruent explanation
reveals:

new symptoms,

a misunderstanding,

an important disagreement,

a safety concern,

or information
the clinician did not receive,

Project DeepDivers
should help route
that information back
into the next consultation.

The resulting structure is:

Individual
↔
translation layer
↔
Clinician

rather than:

Clinician
→
Individual

or:

Mentor
replacing Clinician.


### Mentor Load Reduction

Without a translation layer,
an experienced Therian mentor
may become responsible for:

explaining the Individual
to the clinician,

explaining the clinician
to the Individual,

reframing treatment,

maintaining trust,

and repairing misunderstandings.

This recreates
mentor role compression.

Project DeepDivers
should absorb
repeatable translation work

so that experienced mentors
are not required
to act as permanent
clinical interpreters.


### Identity-Congruent Clinical Translation Requirements

A core clinical-translation principle is:

**Clinical correctness
and communicative usability
are separate requirements.**

A second principle is:

**Do not require clinicians
to become Therian mentors,

and do not require
Therian mentors
to become clinicians.**

A third principle is:

**Preserve the clinical core;
adapt the explanatory frame.**

A fourth principle is:

**Successful referral
is not sufficient
if the resulting information
cannot be meaningfully used.**


### Minimum Translation Packet

A practical consultation summary
should be able to separate:

**Identity context**

How the individual
describes their Therian,
Otherkin,
or related identity.

**Reported experience**

What the person actually
feels,
perceives,
thinks,
or does.

**Interpretation**

How the person currently
understands the experience.

This may be:

psychological,
spiritual,
cultural,
mixed,
or unresolved.

**Current concern**

Why support is being sought now.

For example:

distress,
sleep difficulty,
trauma symptoms,
family conflict,
attention problems,
unsafe behavior,
depression,
anxiety,
or another specific concern.

**Functional impact**

What has changed
in ordinary life,
if anything.

**Safety concerns**

Whether there are
immediate risks
requiring separate escalation.

These fields should remain distinct.


### Identity-Neutral Clinical Description

When appropriate,
an experience may be described
in neutral phenomenological language
without erasing
the individual's own terminology.

For example:

community language
may be recorded first,

followed by a plain description
of what the person reports experiencing.

This allows the clinician
to evaluate the relevant concern
without requiring
the individual to abandon
their own identity framework.


### Pathologization Boundary

Therian identity itself
should not automatically be presented
as the symptom requiring treatment.

Questions should instead focus on:

whether the individual is distressed,

whether functioning is impaired,

whether safety is compromised,

and whether another condition
may require assessment.

At the same time,
fear of pathologization
should not prevent
genuine psychiatric,
psychological,
developmental,
or medical concerns
from being assessed.

The purpose of translation
is to reduce
both forms of error.


### Clinical Non-Indication Does Not End Support

A clinician may determine
that no psychiatric diagnosis,
medical treatment,
or other clinical intervention
is currently required.

Such a conclusion
does not automatically mean
that the individual
no longer wants or benefits from support.

An individual may continue to seek:

acceptance,

reassurance,

identity-respecting conversation,

community belonging,

Unconditional Positive Regard,

or an ongoing supportive relationship.

These needs should not be
artificially converted
into psychiatric symptoms
merely because they are important.

They may instead belong
to the domain of:

peer support,

community support,

mentorship,

friendship,

or other non-clinical
relational support.


### Support Need Is Not the Same as Clinical Need

Project DeepDivers should distinguish:

**clinical need**

from

**relational support need.**

A person may require
neither diagnosis nor treatment

while still benefiting from
being understood,
accepted,
accompanied,
and supported.

Likewise,
the absence of clinical pathology
should not be treated
as evidence that:

Therian identity is unimportant,

identity-related distress never occurs,

community support is unnecessary,

or the individual
should simply manage alone.

The appropriate support layer
depends on the problem being addressed,
not on whether a psychiatric diagnosis exists.


### Boundary of Unconditional Positive Regard

Unconditional Positive Regard
should not mean
unconditional agreement
with every interpretation or behavior.

A supporter may accept
the individual as a person

and respect
their non-human self-understanding

while still:

expressing uncertainty,

setting interpersonal boundaries,

addressing unsafe behavior,

or recommending additional help
when appropriate.

Acceptance of the individual
and evaluation of a specific claim
should remain separable.


### Diagnostic Humility

A community mentor
or LLM
should not tell a clinician:

"this is definitely not
a psychiatric condition"

when that conclusion
has not been professionally established.

Likewise,
the system should not tell
the individual:

"your Therian identity
is caused by a psychiatric disorder"

without appropriate evidence.

The safer formulation is:

the identity context exists,

the reported symptoms or difficulties exist,

and their relationship,
if any,
may require careful assessment.


### Consultation Bandwidth Constraint

Clinical communication
takes place under finite
time and attention constraints.

An individual may have:

a long identity history,

multiple overlapping concerns,

unfamiliar community terminology,

previous healthcare experiences,

and substantial emotional context

that cannot realistically
be explained in full
during a single consultation.

The problem is therefore
not only translation accuracy,

but also:

**translation under limited bandwidth.**

Project DeepDivers should help
the individual distinguish:

what must be communicated now,

what would be useful
if time permits,

and what can remain
in a longer background record
for later reference.

A consultation summary
should therefore prioritize:

the current reason for seeking help,

immediate distress or safety concerns,

functional impact,

communication needs,

and only the identity context
necessary to understand
the current problem.

More extensive Therian terminology,
personal history,
and interpretive context
may remain available
as supplementary material.

The goal is not
to compress the individual
into a diagnostic summary.

The goal is
to prevent limited consultation time
from forcing the individual
to choose between:

explaining their identity

and

receiving help
for the problem
that brought them there.


### Pre-Consultation Scaffolding

Before contacting a professional,
Project DeepDivers may help
the individual prepare:

a concise summary,

important terminology,

the reason for seeking help,

questions they want answered,

communication difficulties,

previous harmful or helpful
healthcare experiences,

and boundaries
they want to communicate.

This may reduce anxiety
and increase the likelihood
that the first consultation
focuses on the actual problem.

a short statement such as:

in my experience."

This is a communication scaffold,
not a universal script.

The individual should be free
to modify or reject it.


### Clinical Literacy Without Clinical Authority

Project DeepDivers
should provide enough
clinical literacy
to help individuals communicate
and navigate services.

It should not claim
clinical authority.

Useful literacy may include:

how to describe distress,

how to distinguish
identity from treatment goals,

how to prepare questions,

how to ask for clarification,

how to request
another professional opinion,

and how to document
what occurred during consultation.

The system should not:

diagnose,

prescribe,

interpret medication effects
as a treating professional,

or determine
that clinical evaluation
is unnecessary.


### Failed Translation Risk

Poor translation may result in:

identity being treated
as the entire clinical problem,

the actual source of distress
being overlooked,

the individual withholding information
because they fear judgment,

the clinician misunderstanding
community terminology literally,

or the individual concluding
that all professional care
is inherently unsafe.

A single poor encounter
may therefore affect
future willingness
to seek help.

Reducing translation failure
is consequently
both a clinical-access issue
and a support-continuity issue.


### Documentation Boundary

Clinical translation documents
may contain sensitive information.

Project DeepDivers should distinguish:

reusable general explanation
of Therianthropy

from

individual consultation summaries
containing private personal information.

The reusable explanation
may be public.

Individual summaries
should remain controlled
by the individual
and shared only
with their consent
except where applicable law
or immediate safety requirements
create different obligations.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core translation principle is:

**Translate context,
do not translate identity away.**

A second principle is:

**Make the problem legible
without making the person
defend their existence first.**

Project DeepDivers
should reduce the amount
of foundational explanation
required from a distressed individual
before useful support can begin.

## FIELD PROBLEM: Pathologization Risk

### Observed Problem

Therian identity
may be interpreted
as evidence of psychiatric disorder
before the individual's
actual distress,
functioning,
safety,
or clinical symptoms
have been separately assessed.

This may occur
when unfamiliar identity language
is treated
as inherently pathological

rather than first being understood
as part of the individual's
self-description.

The central risk is:

**identity becoming the diagnosis
before the actual problem
has been identified.**


### Pathologization Is Not the Same as Assessment

Clinical assessment
is not inherently pathologizing.

A clinician may appropriately assess:

distress,

functional impairment,

reality testing,

mood,

trauma-related symptoms,

attention,

perception,

sleep,

risk,

or other relevant concerns.

Pathologization occurs
when the identity itself
is prematurely treated
as the pathological condition

without sufficient evidence
that the identity,
rather than another problem,
is what requires clinical intervention.


### Terminology Collision

Clinical literature
has historically used terms such as:

clinical lycanthropy,

clinical zoanthropy,

and clinical therianthropy

for psychiatric phenomena
involving delusional beliefs
of transformation into an animal.

Community Therianthropy
uses similar language
for a different identity framework.

These overlapping terms
create a substantial
translation hazard.

A clinician encountering
the word "therianthropy"
through psychiatric literature

and a community member
encountering the same word
through Therian communities

may therefore begin
from very different meanings.

Project DeepDivers
should explicitly distinguish:

**community Therianthropy**

from

**clinical therianthropy /
clinical zoanthropy terminology
used in psychiatric literature.**

Terminological similarity
should not be treated
as evidence
that the underlying phenomena
are equivalent.


### Current External Literature Boundary

A 2025 systematic review
of clinical therianthropy
examined published cases
involving delusional beliefs
of animal transformation.

The same authors also discussed
non-clinical Therians,
Furries,
Otherkin,
and other forms
of animal identification,

and explicitly cautioned
against unnecessarily pathologizing
those non-clinical groups.

The authors additionally proposed
a broader diagnostic spectrum.

That proposal should be preserved
as the position
of those authors,

not as established consensus
that community Therianthropy
belongs on a psychiatric spectrum.

The existence
of such competing frameworks
reinforces the need
for careful terminology
and diagnostic humility.


### Sources of Pathologization Risk

Pathologization may arise from:

unfamiliarity
with Therian terminology,

literal interpretation
of community language,

terminology overlap
with psychiatric literature,

assumption that
unusual identity
implies disorder,

confusion between
spiritual interpretation
and loss of reality testing,

confusion between
Shift descriptions
and delusional transformation,

provider stigma,

or crisis encounters
in which the clinician
first meets the individual
during severe distress.

These are possible pathways.

They should not be interpreted
as evidence that
all clinicians
respond in this manner.


### Identity Does Not Establish Causation

The coexistence of:

Therian identity

and

psychological or psychiatric difficulty

does not by itself establish
that one caused the other.

Possible relationships include:

no causal relationship,

distress arising
from social rejection
or stigma,

another condition
affecting the person's experience,

the identity providing
coping,
meaning,
or autonomy,

multiple interacting factors,

or a relationship
that remains uncertain.

The system should preserve
that uncertainty
unless stronger evidence exists.


### Stigma and Help-Seeking Risk

If an individual expects
their identity
to be automatically treated
as pathology,

they may:

withhold information,

avoid professional care,

minimize symptoms,

delay referral,

or disengage
after a negative encounter.

Provider stigma
can therefore become
an access problem
as well as
an interpersonal problem.

Project DeepDivers
should not assume
that fear of pathologization
is always justified,

but it should treat
that fear
as relevant information
when preparing a referral.


### External Research Context

Existing Therian research
does not support
a simple model
in which Therian identity
is automatically equivalent
to psychiatric disorder.

A 2019 study
of 112 self-identified Therians
reported differences
in some wellbeing-related
and psychological measures,

but also discussed
possible social and stigma-related explanations

and reported findings
consistent with Therian identity
having a potentially protective role
for some participants
on particular measures.

This study
should not be generalized
to all Therians.

It nevertheless illustrates
why correlation,
identity,
distress,
and causation
must remain separate.


### Pathologization Feedback Loop

Pathologization
may create a feedback loop:

identity disclosed

→ identity interpreted
as pathology

→ individual feels misunderstood

→ information is withheld
or support is rejected

→ clinician receives
less accurate context

→ misunderstanding increases

→ future help-seeking
becomes more difficult.

A failed clinical encounter
may therefore create
later referral resistance.

This risk connects directly
to:

Clinical Translation,

External Referral Bottleneck,

and Relational Support
and Continuity.


### Clinical Non-Indication

If assessment finds
no relevant clinical disorder
or treatment need,

the appropriate conclusion
is not necessarily:

"nothing matters here."

The individual may still benefit from:

peer support,

community belonging,

identity exploration,

Unconditional Positive Regard,

or other non-clinical
relational support.

Clinical non-indication
should therefore redirect
support to the appropriate layer,

not erase
the individual's identity
or support needs.


### Anti-Pathologization Is Not Anti-Clinical

Avoiding unnecessary pathologization
does not mean
rejecting psychiatry,
psychology,
medicine,
or diagnosis.

A genuine disorder
may coexist
with Therian identity.

A person may appropriately receive:

psychotherapy,

psychiatric treatment,

medical treatment,

neurodevelopmental assessment,

trauma support,

or crisis intervention

without treating
Therian identity itself
as the target
of elimination.

The correct target
depends on
the actual source
of distress,
impairment,
or danger.


### Mentor and LLM Boundary

A community mentor
or LLM
should not respond
to fear of pathologization
by promising:

"no clinician
will consider this pathological"

or

"this can never
be related
to a psychiatric condition."

Such claims exceed
the support role.

Instead,
the system may help
the individual distinguish:

identity,

experience,

interpretation,

symptoms,

distress,

and treatment goals.

This allows
the individual
to seek assessment

without requiring
either automatic pathologization

or automatic clinical exemption.


### What Should Not Be Generalized

The current evidence
does not establish that:

Therian identity
is a psychiatric disorder,

Therian identity
can never coexist
with psychiatric disorder,

all clinicians
pathologize Therianthropy,

all negative healthcare experiences
result from stigma,

all distress
experienced by Therians
is caused by social rejection,

or Therian identity
is necessarily beneficial
or harmful
to mental health.

Evidence remains limited.

Individual assessment
and careful distinction
between identity
and clinical concern
are required.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core anti-pathologization principle is:

**Assess distress,
function,
and safety
without making identity
the diagnosis by default.**

A second principle is:

**Unusual identity
is not sufficient evidence
of disorder.**

A third principle is:

**Avoiding pathologization
must not become
diagnostic blindness.**

Project DeepDivers
should therefore protect
both:

identity-respecting support

and

access to appropriate
clinical assessment
when genuinely needed.

## FIELD PROBLEM: Diagnostic Overshadowing

### Observed Problem

A highly salient identity,
diagnosis,
or explanatory framework
may cause other problems
to be overlooked.

Within Therian support,
a possible failure pattern is:

a difficulty is noticed

→ it appears related
to Therian identity
or a Shift experience

→ the Therian explanation
becomes the default explanation

→ other psychological,
psychiatric,
developmental,
social,
or medical possibilities
receive insufficient attention.

The central risk is:

**the identity explanation
overshadowing
another problem
that also requires attention.**


### Terminology Boundary

"Diagnostic overshadowing"
is an established clinical term.

It has historically been used
for situations in which
symptoms of another condition
are incorrectly attributed
to an existing diagnosis
or highly salient clinical characteristic.

Its application
to Therian community support
in this document
is an analytical extension.

Current evidence
does not establish
a Therian-specific
diagnostic-overshadowing phenomenon
at population level.

The term is used here
to identify
a plausible support-system failure mode.


### The Reverse Error of Pathologization

Pathologization Risk
and Diagnostic Overshadowing
represent different
but complementary errors.

**Pathologization error**

Therian identity
→ assumed to be pathology.

**Overshadowing error**

distress,
symptoms,
or functional difficulty

→ assumed to be explained
by Therian identity.

The first error
medicalizes identity.

The second error
may hide
a separate condition
or source of distress.

Project DeepDivers
should protect against both.


### Identity Does Not Explain Everything

Therian identity
may be relevant
to an individual's experience

without explaining
every difficulty
the individual encounters.

For example,
an individual may simultaneously experience:

Therian identity,

Species Dysphoria,

family conflict,

trauma-related distress,

attention difficulties,

depression or anxiety,

sleep problems,

physical illness,

neurodevelopmental differences,

social isolation,

or other unrelated concerns.

The presence
of Therian identity
should not determine
in advance
which explanation is correct.


### Shift Attribution Risk

Shift terminology
may create
a particular overshadowing risk.

An individual
or community supporter
may describe:

changes in attention,

impulsivity,

aggression,

fear responses,

dissociation-like experiences,

sensory experiences,

sleep-related experiences,

or changes in behavior

as part of a Shift.

That description
may accurately reflect
the individual's subjective experience.

However,
the label alone
does not establish
the mechanism
producing the experience.

Where distress,
impairment,
danger,
or unusual change
is significant,

other possible explanations
may still require consideration.


### Community-Side Overshadowing

Diagnostic overshadowing
should not be treated
as a clinician-only problem.

Community supporters
may also create
an overshadowing effect
when they interpret
too many difficulties
through Therian terminology.

Possible patterns include:

"that is just a Mental Shift,"

"that is simply
your animal instinct,"

"that reaction happens
because of your Theriotype,"

or

"you do not need
another explanation."

Such statements
may feel identity-affirming,

but they can become harmful
if they prematurely close
further assessment.

Identity affirmation
should not require
causal certainty.


### Clinical-Side Overshadowing

A clinician
may produce
a different form
of overshadowing.

After learning
that an individual
identifies as Therian,

the clinician may focus
so strongly
on the unusual identity language
that the actual reason
for seeking care
receives insufficient attention.

For example,
the presenting problem
may involve:

trauma,

depression,

anxiety,

attention,

sleep,

family safety,

physical symptoms,

or another concern

that is not adequately explained
by the identity itself.

Clinical Translation
should therefore keep
the reason for consultation
visible and separate
from the identity context.


### Bidirectional Misclassification Risk

Project DeepDivers
should recognize
a bidirectional problem.

One error is:

**ordinary or identity-related
Therian experience
→ misclassified
as clinical pathology.**

The opposite error is:

**genuine clinical,
developmental,
social,
or medical difficulty
→ misclassified
as merely Therian experience.**

Neither direction
should be treated
as the safer default.

The correct response
may remain uncertain
until sufficient information
is available.


### The ADHD / Animal-Behavior Question

The author has raised
a specific field question:

whether behaviors described
within human clinical frameworks,
such as attention difficulty
or impulsivity,

could sometimes overlap
with experiences
that a Therian interprets
through animal-associated identity
or behavior.

This question
should remain open.

Current material
does not establish that:

ADHD-like behavior
is actually Therian behavior,

Therian behavior
is being systematically
misdiagnosed as ADHD,

or ADHD diagnoses
in Therians
are invalid.

The reverse risk
must also be preserved:

genuine ADHD
or another condition
could be overlooked

if all relevant behavior
is attributed
to Therian identity.

The appropriate principle is:

**translate first,
then assess;
neither normalize
nor explain away.**


### Trauma and Distress Boundary

The same distinction
applies to trauma-related distress.

A person's trauma history
may affect:

threat perception,

emotional regulation,

social trust,

avoidance,

sleep,

or behavior.

Therian identity
may also influence
how those experiences
are described
or understood.

The existence
of one framework
does not invalidate
the other.

Support should therefore avoid
assuming either:

"this is all trauma"

or

"this is all Therianthropy."

Multiple explanatory layers
may coexist.


### Change From Baseline

A useful safety question
is not only:

"Is this behavior
associated with Therian experience?"

but also:

**"Has something changed?"**

A new,
rapidly worsening,
or unusually intense experience

may warrant
additional attention

even when similar experiences
have previously been understood
through Therian terminology.

Change from
the individual's own baseline
may be more informative
than whether
a community label exists.


### Functional and Safety Check

When uncertainty exists,
Project DeepDivers
should return
to practical questions:

Is the individual distressed?

Has ordinary functioning changed?

Is sleep substantially affected?

Is the individual
able to exercise agency
over their actions?

Is anyone unsafe?

Is the experience
new or rapidly worsening?

Is there another explanation
that should remain open?

These questions
do not diagnose.

They prevent
one explanatory framework
from prematurely closing
the assessment process.


### Uncertainty Is an Acceptable Outcome

A supporter,
LLM,
or individual
does not always need
to determine immediately
whether an experience is:

Therian-related,

psychological,

psychiatric,

neurodevelopmental,

medical,

situational,

or some combination.

"It may involve
more than one factor"

or

"the relationship
is currently unclear"

may be
the most accurate answer.

Project DeepDivers
should treat preserved uncertainty
as safer than
false explanatory closure.


### Consultation Translation

When preparing
for professional consultation,
the individual
should not need
to choose between:

hiding Therian interpretation

and

presenting Therian interpretation
as the only possible cause.

A translation packet

**Community interpretation**

"What happens
is the following..."

"This becomes difficult
because..."

**Open clinical question**

"I would like help determining
whether anything else
may be contributing."

This preserves
the individual's framework

without requiring
the clinician
to accept it
as the sole causal explanation.


### Mentor and LLM Boundary

A mentor or LLM
may help identify
when another explanation
should remain open.

It should not
independently decide:

that a psychiatric diagnosis exists,

that a diagnosis
is definitely incorrect,

that an experience
is definitely caused
by Therian identity,

or that clinical assessment
is unnecessary.

The appropriate role is:

description,

differentiation,

uncertainty preservation,

support,

and navigation.


### What Should Not Be Generalized

The current evidence
does not establish that:

Therian communities
systematically overlook
mental-health conditions,

clinicians systematically
misattribute symptoms
because of Therian identity,

community interpretations
are generally incorrect,

clinical interpretations
are generally more correct,

or every unusual experience
requires professional assessment.

The Therian-specific model
in this section
is primarily
a safety-oriented analytical framework.

External research
specific to Therian populations
remains limited.

A core anti-overshadowing principle is:

**No single identity,
diagnosis,
or explanatory framework
should automatically explain
every problem.**

A second principle is:

**Preserve the individual's
meaning
without prematurely closing
causal inquiry.**

A third principle is:

**Translate,

neither normalize

Project DeepDivers
should help keep
multiple possibilities visible

until there is
sufficient reason
to narrow them.

## FIELD PROBLEM: External Referral Bottleneck

### Observed Problem

Recognizing
that professional support
may be appropriate

does not guarantee
that appropriate support
can actually be reached.

Referral may fail
at multiple stages:

identifying a suitable service,

making first contact,

communicating the problem,

obtaining an appointment,

affording continued access,

finding a provider
with relevant competence,

or sustaining participation
long enough
for support to become useful.

The resulting problem is not only:

"Where should this person be referred?"

It is also:

**"Can this referral
actually become usable support?"**


### Referral Is a Process, Not an Instruction

"See a professional"

is not
a complete handoff.

A usable referral may require:

service discovery,

eligibility checking,

translation,

contact preparation,

appointment access,

communication support,

and follow-up.

Failure at any stage
may return the individual
to the original support system

without resolving
the underlying problem.


### Availability Is Not Accessibility

The existence
of an appropriate professional
or service

does not mean
that the individual
can realistically access it.

Possible barriers include:

financial cost,

waiting time,

limited appointment availability,

travel or geographic distance,

work or school obligations,

communication difficulty,

lack of culturally
or identity-informed providers,

previous harmful experiences,

fear or stigma,

and the practical effort
required to repeatedly seek help.

Referral should therefore distinguish:

**a service exists**

from

**the individual can use it.**


### System Capacity Is a Support Variable

Referral failure
should not automatically
be interpreted
as unwillingness,
noncompliance,
or lack of motivation
on the part of the individual.

Healthcare,
mental-health,
social-support,
and crisis systems
operate under finite:

time,

staffing,

specialist availability,

appointment capacity,

geographic coverage,

and financial resources.

An appropriate provider
may be difficult to reach

even when:

the individual wants help,

the mentor identifies
an appropriate route,

and the referral itself
is conceptually correct.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore treat
system capacity
as part of
the support environment.


### Appropriate Does Not Mean Available

A professional
may appear clinically suitable

while remaining
operationally unavailable.

For example,
the service may:

not accept new clients,

have a long waiting period,

be unaffordable,

require travel
that is not realistic,

lack remote access,

have age or eligibility restrictions,

or be inaccessible
because of communication needs.

A theoretically ideal referral
may therefore be less useful

than a sufficiently appropriate
and realistically accessible
alternative.


### Age and Eligibility Barriers

Age may affect access
to both formal services
and community support.

A resource may exist
and may even be appropriate,

while remaining unavailable
because the individual
does not satisfy
an age or eligibility requirement.

For minors,
possible barriers may include:

minimum-age rules,

parental or guardian involvement,

safeguarding requirements,

service eligibility,

limited ability
to travel independently,

financial dependence,

and fear that seeking help
may disclose information
to family or institutions.

These restrictions
may exist for legitimate
legal or safeguarding reasons.

However,
their protective purpose
does not eliminate
the support gap
experienced by the individual.


### Information Access Is Not Relational Access

An age-restricted community
may allow a young person
to read public information

while preventing
interactive participation.

This may preserve
informational access

without providing:

belonging,

peer interaction,

reassurance,

identity-respecting dialogue,

Unconditional Positive Regard,

or an ongoing supportive relationship.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore distinguish:

**access to information**

from

**access to relational support.**


### Age Restriction and Circumvention Risk

SheilaGrace reports
having encountered
multiple underage individuals
who attempted
to bypass community age restrictions.

This observation
should be preserved
as author field testimony.

Current Therian community records
also document cases
of underage users
attempting to falsify age
in order to gain community access.

The existence
of circumvention attempts
does not mean
that age restrictions
should simply be removed.

It indicates
that unmet belonging
and support needs
may persist
outside the protected community boundary.

A safer system
should therefore ask:

**What support remains available
when admission
to the preferred community
is not appropriate?**


### Older-Participant Access Barrier

Age barriers
do not operate
only toward minors.

An older individual
may technically be eligible
to participate

while still finding
a community difficult to use
because of:

large generational differences,

platform culture,

communication style,

rapidly changing community norms,

age-skewed membership,

or discomfort
with entering spaces
dominated by much younger participants.

This is not
formal exclusion.

It is a form
of practical
or social accessibility barrier.

Project DeepDivers
should not assume
that membership eligibility
means that a community
is equally usable
for every age group.


### Cross-Age Accessible Support Infrastructure

One possible role
for Project DeepDivers
is to provide
an informational
and conversational layer

that remains accessible
across different age groups

when an appropriate peer community
cannot easily be reached.

Accessibility across age groups
should not mean
identical treatment
regardless of age.

The system should adapt
safety boundaries,
privacy expectations,
communication style,
and escalation behavior

to the individual's
age and circumstances.

Such a layer may provide:

terminology,

identity exploration,

structured self-description,

reference navigation,

preparation for human support,

and nonjudgmental conversation.

However,
an LLM should not become
a substitute
for safeguarding,
trusted human relationships,
professional care,
or age-appropriate
social support.

For minors especially,
privacy,
safety,
dependency,
and escalation boundaries
require explicit protection.


### Causal Caution

The author has observed
that some young people
enter Therian-related spaces
while experiencing:

bullying,

family conflict,

abuse,

neglect,

social isolation,

or unmet belonging needs.

The author has also proposed
that such environments
may contribute
to the development
of Copinglink identities
for some individuals.

This should remain
an author hypothesis.

The current audit
does not establish
that adversity
causes Copinglink identity,

nor that young people
seeking Therian community
are necessarily
using identity for coping.

The relevant design problem
is the coexistence
of identity exploration

and

unmet safety,
belonging,
or relational needs.


### First-Contact Friction

The first contact itself
may become a barrier.

An individual may need to:

identify the correct service,

understand
how to make contact,

explain the problem
to unfamiliar staff,

repeat personal history,

describe Therian identity
without knowing
how it will be received,

and tolerate uncertainty
about what happens next.

For some individuals,
these tasks may be
more difficult
than recognizing
that help is needed.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore support
the transition
between:

"help exists"

and

"I can actually begin
using that help."


### Handoff Scaffolding

Where appropriate,
Project DeepDivers may help prepare:

a concise consultation summary,

the current reason
for seeking support,

relevant safety
or functional concerns,

communication preferences,

identity-context explanation,

questions for the provider,

and a short first-contact message.

This support
should reduce
avoidable communication burden.

It should not become
informal diagnosis,
case management,
or professional treatment.


### Referral Deadlock

A referral deadlock occurs
when:

the current supporter
cannot safely provide
the full required support,

but the individual
cannot or will not
successfully transition
to another support route.

Possible causes include:

access barriers,

fear,

communication difficulty,

previous harmful experiences,

provider mismatch,

service refusal,

or lack of suitable services.

The existence
of referral deadlock

does not mean
that the current mentor
must indefinitely assume
the missing professional role.

It indicates
that the support pathway itself
has failed
and requires another route.


### Referral Refusal Is Not One Phenomenon

An individual
may decline a referral
for different reasons.

Possible explanations include:

the service is genuinely inaccessible,

the service feels unsafe,

previous care was harmful,

communication demands
are overwhelming,

the individual
does not understand
why referral is being suggested,

the proposed service
does not match
the actual problem,

or the individual
does not currently want
that form of support.

These possibilities
should not be collapsed
into:

"the person refuses help."

Understanding the barrier
may change
the next appropriate step.


### Failed Handoff Does Not End Support

A failed referral
should not automatically mean:

the individual
must return
to unsupported isolation,

the mentor
must become
the treating professional,

or the individual
has become
"too difficult to help."

Where safety permits,
the support system
may return temporarily
to:

informational support,

relational support,

another referral route,

or renewed preparation
for future contact.

A failed handoff
is information
about the support pathway.

It is not proof
that the individual
is unsupportable.


### Clinical Non-Indication and Return Path

A professional may also determine
that no clinical treatment
is currently indicated.

This should not be treated
as referral failure.

The result may instead indicate
that the individual's needs
belong primarily to:

community support,

identity exploration,

peer support,

informational support,

or relational support.

The system should therefore provide
a return path
from clinical assessment

to non-clinical support

without framing the individual
as rejected,
invalid,
or untreated.


### No Wrong Door Principle

An individual
should not need
to identify
the perfect support layer
before asking for help.

A sustainable system
should allow movement between:

self-directed information,

peer support,

mentorship,

clinical consultation,

social support,

and crisis services

without treating
every routing correction
as failure.

The goal is not
perfect first placement.

The goal is:

**recoverable routing.**


### Jurisdiction Layer

Referral systems
vary by:

country,

region,

age,

insurance or payment system,

service availability,

legal framework,

and safeguarding structure.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore separate:

**conceptual referral guidance**

from

**jurisdiction-specific implementation.**

The conceptual layer
should explain:

when referral may be useful,

what information
should be prepared,

and what makes
a handoff usable.

Regional directories
should explain:

where support
can currently be accessed.


### Regional Directory Requirements

A regional referral entry
should ideally record:

jurisdiction,

service purpose,

eligibility,

access method,

cost information
where available,

remote or in-person availability,

known operating limitations,

source,

and last verification date.

Time-sensitive service information
should remain outside
long-lived canonical guidance.


### Mentor Boundary

A mentor
may help:

identify possible routes,

prepare communication,

explain Therian context,

and remain relationally supportive
during referral.

A mentor
should not be required
to compensate indefinitely
for systemic service shortages

by becoming:

a clinician,

case manager,

crisis worker,

or permanent substitute
for inaccessible professional care.


### LLM Boundary

An LLM may assist with:

information gathering,

service comparison,

communication preparation,

translation,

question drafting,

and organization
of referral information.

It should not guarantee
that:

a particular provider
will be appropriate,

a service
will accept the individual,

treatment
will be effective,

or a referral
will successfully complete.

Time-sensitive information
should be source-checked
and visibly dated.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core referral principle is:

**Availability
is not accessibility.**

A second principle is:

**A correct referral
that cannot be used
is not a completed handoff.**

A third principle is:

**A failed handoff
should preserve
a return path to support.**

Project DeepDivers
should therefore function
not only as a directory,

but as:

preparation infrastructure,

translation infrastructure,

navigation infrastructure,

and continuity infrastructure.

## FIELD PROBLEM: Mentor Role Overload

### Observed Problem

SheilaGrace reports
that experienced Therian community members
could gradually accumulate responsibilities
far beyond ordinary peer mentorship.

This was not necessarily
a deliberate organizational decision.

Rather,
roles accumulated because
unmet needs had nowhere else to go.

When terminology resources
were insufficient,
the mentor became a terminology interpreter.

When an individual was distressed,
the mentor became an emotional supporter.

When behavior created safety concerns,
the mentor became a behavioral-safety advisor.

When professional care was difficult to access,
the mentor became a clinical translator
and referral navigator.

When outsiders misunderstood Therianthropy,
the mentor could also become
a public-facing representative.

The resulting problem may be described as:

**role compression**

— multiple distinct support functions
becoming concentrated
in one experienced individual.


### Evidence in Earlier Support Material

The 2024 support material itself
documents substantial role compression.

It expected supporters
to address or assist with:

- unconditional acceptance and trust-building,
- differentiation between Therianthropy
  and Clinical Lycanthropy,
- behavioral problems associated
  with some Shift experiences,
- Species Dysphoria,
- constructive expression
  of animal-associated urges,
- difficult family or school environments,
- identification of external support organizations,
- evaluation of whether those organizations
  might be appropriate,
- community referral,
- psychological support,
- behavioral self-regulation,
- and ongoing management
  of social functioning.

The source also used
animal-rescue,
surrogate-parent,
and animal-training analogies
to describe parts of the support process.

It later proposed
an author-developed intervention,
Threat Level Correction Therapy (TLCT),
as a method for reducing
perceived overreactions to threat.

These elements demonstrate
how a community support role
had expanded into areas resembling:

peer support,
behavioral coaching,
case navigation,
mental-health guidance,
and intervention design.

This section preserves that expansion
as historical evidence.

It does not endorse
all earlier methods
as clinically established
or currently appropriate.


### Author Retrospective Assessment

SheilaGrace now describes
the practical role
as resembling several professions
being combined into one person.

Author metaphors include:

psychiatrist,
animal trainer,
veterinarian,
rehabilitation supporter,
translator,
historian,
and diplomat.

These terms should not be read literally.

They express
the breadth of functions
that the author felt required
to keep individuals
and community spaces functioning.

In particular,
the "animal trainer" analogy
reflected an attempt
to respect the individual's
non-human self-understanding
while teaching safer behavior.

Current analysis should prefer
terms such as:

behavioral self-regulation,
harm reduction,
and agency-preserving support

rather than framing
the individual themselves
as an object to be trained.


### Role Accretion Mechanism

Mentor overload may emerge
through a cumulative process:

an unresolved informational need
→ mentor explanation

an unresolved identity question
→ mentor interpretation

distress or interpersonal difficulty
→ mentor emotional support

unsafe or socially costly behavior
→ mentor behavioral guidance

lack of suitable professional care
→ mentor informal psychological guidance

difficulty accessing services
→ mentor referral navigation

institutional misunderstanding
→ mentor clinical translation

public misunderstanding
→ mentor reputation management

historical uncertainty
→ mentor archival reconstruction

None of these tasks alone
necessarily creates overload.

The risk emerges
when the same individual
becomes the default endpoint
for most or all of them.


### Expertise Boundary Problem

Community experience
does not automatically provide
professional competence
in every adjacent field.

A mentor may possess
substantial knowledge
of Therian terminology,
history,
or lived experience
while lacking formal training in:

clinical psychology,
psychiatry,
social work,
crisis intervention,
medicine,
or behavioral science.

Conversely,
a qualified clinician
may possess relevant professional expertise
while knowing little
about Therianthropy.

The support problem therefore cannot be solved
simply by declaring either group
sufficient on its own.

A translation and referral layer
may be required between them.


### Harm and Error Risk Within Role Overload

When a mentor operates
outside their knowledge boundary,
good intentions
do not eliminate the possibility of harm.

SheilaGrace reports
having sought psychological knowledge
because both personal need
and the needs of friends
made such knowledge appear necessary.

The author also retrospectively recognizes
that incomplete or self-taught knowledge
may sometimes have produced
unhelpful or harmful guidance.

This testimony should be preserved
without converting it
into a claim that harm occurred
in every case.

The important design lesson is:

a support system
should not require
a volunteer mentor
to improvise professional practice
because no alternative route exists.


### Mentor Welfare Risk

Role overload can affect
the mentor as well as
the person seeking support.

Possible consequences include:

exhaustion,
chronic sense of responsibility,
difficulty disengaging,
fear of making mistakes,
pressure to remain constantly available,
and continuation of support
beyond the mentor's actual capacity.

An experienced mentor
may also become
a repository of community knowledge.

If that individual becomes unavailable,
both relational support
and accumulated information
may disappear simultaneously.

This creates
both a welfare risk
and a knowledge-continuity risk.


### Seeker Dependency Risk

Role concentration may also encourage
dependency on a particular mentor.

If one individual becomes
the main source of:

identity validation,
terminology,
emotional support,
behavior guidance,
clinical translation,
and community access,

disagreement,
absence,
burnout,
or relationship rupture
can become disproportionately disruptive.

Reducing dependency
does not require
making mentorship impersonal.

It requires ensuring
that essential knowledge
and navigation paths
do not exist only
inside one relationship.


### What Should Not Be Generalized

The current evidence
does not establish that:

all Therian mentors
perform these roles,

all Therian communities
have the same support structure,

all support seekers
require psychological care,

community mentorship
is inherently harmful,

or professional services
can automatically replace
community knowledge.

The role-overload model
primarily reflects
SheilaGrace's field experience
and earlier support material.

Its broader prevalence
requires additional evidence.


### Design Implications

Project DeepDivers
should reduce role compression
rather than attempting
to create more capable
all-purpose mentors.

Functions should be separated
where possible.

Repeatable informational work
can be supported by documentation
or structured tools.

Identity exploration
can use self-directed
and peer-supported resources.

Experienced mentors
can contribute contextual
and lived-experience knowledge.

Clinical problems
should be routed
toward appropriately qualified support
where such support is available.

Clinical translation resources
should reduce the need
for either the Therian
or the mentor
to explain the entire subject
from the beginning
during every referral.

Public communication resources
should similarly reduce
the expectation
that individual mentors
must repeatedly defend
or represent Therianthropy
to outsiders.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core architectural requirement is:

**Do not build a better overloaded mentor.**

Build an environment
in which fewer tasks
require one mentor
in the first place.

The system should preserve
human mentorship
for functions
where relationship,
judgment,
experience,
and context
are genuinely valuable.

Other functions should be:

documented,
distributed,
translated,
automated where safe,
or referred where appropriate.

## FIELD PROBLEM: Mentor Harm and Error Risk

### Observed Problem

Community mentors may cause harm
even when their intention
is to help.

Risk increases when a mentor
is expected to respond
to problems outside
their actual training or expertise,
especially when
no realistic alternative support route
is available.

Good intentions,
community experience,
and personal familiarity
with Therianthropy
do not by themselves guarantee
that psychological,
clinical,
behavioral,
or crisis-related guidance
will be safe or accurate.


### Author Testimony

SheilaGrace reports
that psychological knowledge
became practically necessary
because of two simultaneous needs:

understanding and supporting herself,

and helping friends
who sought emotional
or identity-related support.

The author did not receive
formal professional education
in psychology,
psychiatry,
or clinical practice.

Much of the knowledge used
in community support
was therefore self-directed
and acquired
under practical pressure.

In retrospect,
the author recognizes
that incomplete knowledge
may sometimes have resulted
in inaccurate,
unhelpful,
or harmful guidance.

This testimony is preserved
as an accountability
and system-design record.

It should not be interpreted
as evidence of malicious intent,
nor as proof that every earlier intervention
caused harm.


### Primary Error Pathways

Potential mentor errors include:

- presenting personal interpretation
  as established community knowledge,

- treating anecdotal patterns
  as universal rules,

- presenting speculative psychological
  or neurological mechanisms
  as established fact,

- confusing Therian identity experience
  with psychiatric pathology,

- overlooking genuine mental-health problems
  because they appear related
  to Therian experience,

- becoming too confident
  in identity classification,

- encouraging behavioral suppression
  when safer expression
  would have been possible,

- failing to recognize
  when a problem exceeded
  the mentor's competence,

- referring too late
  because an appropriate referral path
  was unclear,

- referring too quickly
  without translating
  the individual's identity context,

- and continuing support
  after exhaustion
  had reduced the mentor's judgment
  or emotional capacity.

These are risk pathways.

They should not be interpreted
as claims that every pathway
occurred in every historical case.


### Knowledge Boundary Failure

A major risk appears
when uncertainty is hidden.

A mentor may feel pressure
to provide an answer
because the individual seeking help
has nowhere else to ask.

Under those conditions:

"I do not know"

may feel emotionally inadequate,

while an uncertain explanation
may gradually become
treated as established knowledge.

Repeated use of such explanations
can then transform
a provisional mentor interpretation
into apparent community doctrine.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore preserve
uncertainty explicitly
rather than treating uncertainty
as a failure of support.


### Identity / Clinical Double Error

Support systems face
a bidirectional error risk.

One direction is:

non-human identity experience
→ interpreted automatically
as psychiatric pathology.

The opposite direction is:

psychological or psychiatric distress
→ interpreted automatically
as merely part of Therian experience.

Both errors can cause harm.

A safer approach is
to separate questions:

What does this experience mean
to the individual?

Is the individual distressed?

Is daily functioning impaired?

Is anyone in danger?

Is specialized assessment
or support needed?

These questions may overlap,
but they should not be collapsed
into a single judgment
about whether the identity itself
is valid.


### Behavioral Error Boundary

Identity validation
does not require
approving every action.

Likewise,
behavioral correction
does not require
rejecting the identity.

A mentor may support
a person's non-human self-understanding
while also helping them consider
the consequences of:

aggression,
unsafe impulsive behavior,
social conflict,
self-injury,
or behavior
that may endanger other people.

The appropriate target
is safer action
and greater agency,

not forced normalization
of identity.


### Error Repair

A healthy support system
must allow earlier guidance
to be corrected.

Correction may include:

acknowledging uncertainty,

revising inaccurate explanations,

distinguishing field observation
from established evidence,

withdrawing advice
that is no longer considered safe,

and documenting
why the interpretation changed.

Correction should not require
pretending the earlier record
never existed.

Where historically useful,
the previous interpretation
should remain recoverable
with its later correction attached.


### Privacy Boundary

Error review
does not require
public disclosure
of every individual case.

Specific incidents
may contain:

private identity information,
mental-health information,
interpersonal conflict,
or details belonging
to other individuals.

The public audit
should preserve
the failure pattern
and design lesson.

Detailed incident records,
if preserved at all,
should remain separate
and access-controlled
unless appropriate consent
allows broader use.


### High-Complexity Support and Referral Deadlock

SheilaGrace reports
having encountered support cases
in which community mentorship
became entangled with:

family conflict,
possible mental-health or neurodevelopmental complexity,
high support expectations,
difficulty communicating with professionals,
resistance to formal services,
and disagreement among informal supporters.

In at least one historical case,
the author advised
seeking medical
and public social-support services.

The individual reportedly showed
substantial resistance
to those referral routes,
partly because communication
with unfamiliar people
was itself experienced
as difficult.

The author was therefore placed
in a difficult position:

the case appeared to exceed
community-mentor capacity,

while the proposed transfer
to external support
did not successfully occur.

This pattern may be described as:

**referral deadlock**

— a condition in which
the current helper
cannot safely provide
the full required support,

but the individual
cannot or will not
transition to
a more appropriate support route.

Referral deadlock
can substantially increase
mentor workload
and may create pressure
to continue supporting
beyond one's competence.


### Competing Informal Authority Risk

The author also reports
conflict arising
when other community participants
presented themselves
as possessing relevant expertise
and proposed additional
community programs or infrastructure.

Such proposals could increase
the mentor's responsibilities
without providing:

formal accountability,
shared operational responsibility,
clear professional boundaries,
or sustainable maintenance.

Disagreement over these projects
could then become
a source of community conflict.

The design lesson is:

recommendations that create
new support obligations
should not automatically become
the responsibility
of the most experienced available mentor.


### Support Withdrawal Boundary

A mentor may reach a point
at which continued support
is no longer sustainable.

Ending or limiting support
in such circumstances
does not automatically mean
that the individual's needs
were unimportant.

A support system should therefore provide:

clear role boundaries,
handoff preparation,
multiple support routes,
documentation continuity,
and a way for mentors
to withdraw safely
without becoming
the sole remaining support node.


### Fail-Safe Requirements

A safer mentor-support system
should make it easier to:

state uncertainty,

identify the source
of a claim,

separate personal experience
from general evidence,

recognize competence boundaries,

seek a second opinion,

pause before giving
high-impact advice,

prepare an individual
for professional consultation,

and escalate
when safety risks exceed
community support capacity.

No single mentor
should need
to be correct every time
for the support system
to remain safe.


### LLM and Project DeepDivers Risk

LLM-based support
can reproduce
many of the same failures
as human mentorship.

It may also amplify them
because fluent language
can make uncertain information
sound authoritative
and because one erroneous explanation
can potentially be repeated
at scale.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore require
LLM support to distinguish:

source-derived information,

community testimony,

author interpretation,

model inference,

and unresolved uncertainty.

An LLM should not
silently convert
a Therian identity question
into a clinical diagnosis.

It should also not
silently convert
a serious clinical problem
into an identity-only explanation.

Where specialized care
is appropriate,
the system should support
translation and preparation
rather than pretending
to replace qualified care.


### What Should Not Be Generalized

The current record
does not establish that:

community mentorship
is inherently unsafe,

self-directed psychological learning
has no value,

every past mistake
caused significant harm,

all mistakes require
public disclosure,

or professional care
is automatically safer
or more Therian-literate
than community support.

The relevant problem
is not the existence
of imperfect helpers.

The problem is a system
that requires imperfect helpers
to operate without boundaries,
backup,
or correction mechanisms.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core safety requirement is:

**Make errors observable,
correctable,
and non-catastrophic.**

The system should not depend
on one mentor,
one interpretation,
or one model output
being permanently correct.

Support infrastructure
should preserve:

uncertainty,
provenance,
revision history,
alternative interpretations,
and safe escalation paths.

A mistaken interpretation
should remain repairable
before it becomes
a person's only available explanation
or a community's inherited doctrine.

## FIELD PROBLEM: Relational Support and Continuity

### Observed Problem

Support needs are not always informational.

Some individuals may already know
what resources exist
or what action would theoretically be appropriate,
but still struggle
to take the next step alone.

In these cases,
the most valued form of support
may be:

encouragement,
reassurance,
continued presence,
emotional steadiness,
and the sense
that someone remains beside them
while they move toward help.


### Author Testimony

SheilaGrace reports
that in at least one difficult historical case,
she was unable to complete
the full support process
or provide all of the practical scaffolding
that would now appear useful.

However,
she was able to provide
continued emotional support
while the individual considered
or approached external help.

This included:

encouragement,
reassurance,
remaining available,
and helping the individual
feel less alone.

The author considers this
an important part
of what was successfully provided.


### Informational Support vs Relational Support

Support should not be reduced
to information delivery.

Two different functions
may be required:

**Informational support**

- explanation,
- navigation,
- referral preparation,
- terminology,
- and practical planning.

**Relational support**

- reassurance,
- encouragement,
- companionship,
- emotional grounding,
- and continuity of contact.

Different individuals
may value these functions differently.

For some,
relational support
may be more immediately important
than additional information.


### Continuity Risk

When relational support
becomes concentrated
in one mentor,
one community,
or one artificial agent,
loss of that relationship
may itself become distressing.

Potential disruptions include:

mentor withdrawal,
community closure,
account loss,
system migration,
model replacement,
memory loss,
or personality discontinuity.

This creates
a continuity risk
separate from ordinary
information loss.


### LLM-Specific Risk

LLM-based support
may become emotionally significant
because it can provide:

consistent availability,
patient listening,
nonjudgmental dialogue,
and repeated reassurance.

This can be beneficial.

However,
the system should not imply
guaranteed permanent continuity,
exclusive attachment,
or irreplaceable presence.

A support system should reduce
the risk that one model instance
becomes the individual's
only emotional support node.


### Design Implications

Project DeepDivers
and Project Undertaker
should preserve
not only information,
but also continuity scaffolding.

Possible mechanisms include:

- durable records of prior support context,
- clear recovery paths after system changes,
- portable summaries,
- relationship-history continuity records,
- explicit handoff procedures,
- support from multiple possible sources,
- and clear distinction
  between emotional support
  and clinical responsibility.

The goal is not
to eliminate meaningful relationships.

The goal is
to make relationship disruption
less catastrophic.


### Project Undertaker Requirement

A key continuity principle is:

**Preserve not only what was known,
but enough relational context
for support to become recognizable again.**

Relationship history
should remain recoverable
without falsely claiming
that perfect identity continuity
is guaranteed.

## FIELD PROBLEM: Crisis Escalation

### Observed Problem

Community support may occasionally encounter
situations in which
ordinary peer support is no longer sufficient.

Possible concerns may involve:

- immediate danger to the individual,
- danger to another person,
- serious injury or acute medical need,
- severe psychological destabilization,
- suspected abuse or neglect,
- inability to maintain basic safety,
- or a rapidly worsening situation
  that exceeds community-support capacity.

The purpose of crisis escalation
is not to diagnose the individual.

The purpose is to recognize
when safety needs
have exceeded the role
of ordinary mentorship.


### Crisis Escalation Is Not Diagnosis

A mentor does not need
to determine:

the correct psychiatric diagnosis,

whether Therianthropy caused the crisis,

whether a Shift caused the crisis,

or whether the individual's identity
is clinically valid

before seeking additional help.

The relevant question is:

**Has the situation exceeded
what peer support can safely contain?**

Safety escalation
should therefore remain separate
from identity judgment.


### External Guidance Model

Current Japanese public-health guidance
for community gatekeepers
emphasizes a sequence resembling:

notice distress,
listen respectfully,
connect the person
with appropriate support,
and continue supportive contact.

The same guidance also recognizes
the importance
of the helper's own wellbeing
and knowing where
the helper can seek assistance.

This model supports
a limited but meaningful role
for community mentors:

presence,
listening,
encouragement,
connection,
and continued relational support.

It does not require
the mentor to become
the treating clinician.


### Minimum Safety Questions

Project DeepDivers
should not attempt
to perform a psychiatric diagnosis
before escalation.

Instead,
a minimal safety screen
may ask practical questions such as:

Is anyone in immediate physical danger?

Does the person appear able
to remain safe
for the immediate future?

Has serious injury
or another acute medical emergency occurred?

Is another person
potentially at immediate risk?

Is a child or dependent person
reporting possible abuse or neglect?

Does the situation require
a level of assessment
that the current supporter
cannot provide?

These questions are intended
for routing,
not diagnosis.


### Escalation Levels

A future support protocol
should distinguish
at least three operational levels.

**Immediate emergency**

The situation involves
an immediate threat
to life,
serious physical safety,
or another acute emergency.

Community discussion
should not become
the primary response channel.

Emergency services
or other appropriate
local emergency systems
should be used.

**Urgent but not clearly immediate**

The individual is significantly distressed,
functioning is deteriorating,
or the supporter has substantial concern,
but there is no clearly identified
immediate emergency.

The goal is
timely connection
with an appropriate
professional or public support service,
with practical assistance
for making that connection.

**Non-emergency support**

The individual is distressed
or struggling
but remains sufficiently safe
for ordinary community support,
planned professional consultation,
and ongoing relational support.

These categories
are routing aids,
not diagnoses.


### Japan-Specific Safeguarding Context

For implementation in Japan,
current official systems include:

emergency medical / rescue response,

police emergency response,

public mental-health consultation,

child-protection consultation,

and regional emergency-medical advice services.

Specific telephone numbers,
operating hours,
and service coverage
should not be treated
as timeless canonical knowledge.

They should be maintained
in a separately verified
regional referral directory
with a visible
last-checked date.


### Child and Dependent-Person Safety

Reports involving
possible child abuse or neglect
require a different support boundary
from ordinary identity mentoring.

A Therian mentor
should not be expected
to investigate
whether an allegation is true.

The role is not
to become an investigator,
family therapist,
or child-protection professional.

The system should instead
surface appropriate
public safeguarding routes
and help the individual
communicate what they report experiencing.

The original statement
should be preserved
as a report by the individual,
not silently upgraded
into a verified factual conclusion.


### Referral Refusal and Crisis

An individual may resist
professional or public support.

Reasons may include:

fear,
communication difficulty,
previous harmful experiences,
lack of trust,
stigma,
or overwhelming anxiety.

Refusal does not automatically mean
that the mentor must take over
the professional role.

Where the situation
is not an immediate emergency,
support may include:

helping prepare what to say,

writing a consultation summary,

explaining what the first contact
may involve,

identifying communication preferences,

and remaining emotionally supportive
during the transition.

Where immediate serious danger exists,
ordinary referral negotiation
may no longer be sufficient
and emergency procedures
may be required.


### Handoff Scaffolding

Project DeepDivers
should make referral
more usable
than simply saying:

"Go see a professional."

A handoff package
may help the individual describe:

what is happening now,

what kind of help they are seeking,

relevant distress
or safety concerns,

what Therianthropy means
in their own experience,

what they do not want
automatically pathologized,

important communication difficulties,

relevant prior treatment experiences,

and questions
they want to ask the professional.

The purpose is
to reduce translation friction.

The package should not
pre-diagnose the individual
or instruct the professional
what diagnosis to make.


### Therianthropy Translation Boundary

During crisis support,
Therian identity
should neither be assumed
to be the cause of the crisis

nor ignored
when it is important
to the individual's experience.

Clinical translation should communicate:

the identity context,

the reported experience,

the distress or safety concern,

and the individual's own interpretation

as separate pieces of information.

This helps reduce
both pathologization
and diagnostic overshadowing.


### Mentor Boundary

A community mentor
may appropriately provide:

listening,
reassurance,
encouragement,
practical preparation,
identity-context translation,
and continued relational support.

A mentor should not be required
to independently provide:

psychiatric diagnosis,
medical treatment,
emergency clinical assessment,
or professional crisis intervention.

Knowing when to escalate
is part of responsible support,
not evidence
that the mentor has failed.


### LLM Boundary

An LLM may assist with:

organizing information,

drafting a message
to a support service,

preparing questions
for a clinician,

explaining Therian terminology,

helping the individual
describe their experience,

and providing
calm relational support.

An LLM should not present itself
as the final authority
on whether a person
is medically safe.

When credible serious danger
is present,
the system should prioritize
connection to appropriate
human emergency
or professional support.


### Mentor Welfare During Crisis

A crisis involving
another individual
can also destabilize
the mentor.

The system should therefore support:

consultation with another helper,

handoff,
temporary withdrawal,

documentation continuity,

and post-crisis decompression.

A mentor should not be expected
to remain continuously available
because another individual's crisis exists.


### Service Directory Architecture

Time-sensitive referral information
should be separated
from the conceptual support manual.

For example:

SupportGuide
→ explains when and why to escalate

Regional Referral Directory
→ explains where to escalate today

Each regional entry
should include:

jurisdiction,
service purpose,
access method,
eligibility where known,
operating limitations,
source,
and last verification date.

This prevents outdated contact information
from becoming embedded
in long-lived canonical guidance.


### Project DeepDivers Requirement

A core crisis-support principle is:

**Escalate safety,
not identity.**

The system should help identify
when the current support layer
is no longer sufficient

without requiring
the individual's Therian identity
to be rejected,
proved,
diagnosed,
or explained away.

A second requirement is:

**Referral is not complete
until the handoff
is realistically usable.**

Project DeepDivers
should therefore support
both escalation

and

the practical transition
into the next layer of care.
