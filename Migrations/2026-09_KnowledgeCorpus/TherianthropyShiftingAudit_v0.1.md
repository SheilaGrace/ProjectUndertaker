# Therianthropy Shifting Audit v0.1

## Purpose

This document audits shifting terminology,
source provenance,
historical usage,
author interpretation,
personal experience,
cultural interpretation,
and empirical or spiritual claims
before creation of canonical Therianthropy reference material.

The original source documents must remain recoverable.

This audit follows two working principles:

**Experience First, Label Second.**

**Experience ≠ Cultural Interpretation ≠ Community Classification.**

A reported experience does not need
to fit a precise Shift subtype
in order to be preserved,
discussed,
or considered meaningful.

When classification is uncertain,
the phenomenology should be preserved first
and the label may remain provisional or unresolved.


## Audit Method

For each Shift term, distinguish:

- Historical evidence
- Therian Guide usage
- Therian Wiki usage
- SheilaGrace source wording
- SheilaGrace author expansion
- Personal / field observation
- Cultural interpretation
- Empirical / clinical claims
- Spiritual / metaphysical claims
- Classification problems
- Canonical candidate
- Verification status


## Cross-Cultural Translation Boundary

Culturally specific experiences
should not automatically be forced
into the nearest available English-language
Therian Shift category.

Where no clean mapping exists,
preserve separately:

1. the reported experience,
2. the individual's cultural interpretation,
3. the possible community terminology mapping.

A failed or uncertain terminology mapping
does not invalidate the original experience.


## Taxonomy Design Boundary

The existence of a term
in a community glossary or wiki
establishes that the term has been documented there.

It does not by itself establish:

- historical importance,
- widespread usage,
- conceptual necessity,
- empirical validity,
- or the need for the term
  to remain a separate canonical category.

Different Shift terms may describe
different kinds of dimensions,
including:

- phenomenology,
- trigger,
- referent,
- interpretation,
- or metaphysical framework.

These dimensions should not automatically
be treated as a single flat taxonomy.


# Initial Audit Set

## TERM: Shift / Shifting

### Historical Evidence

Early community material shows that shifting terminology
was already developing during the 1990s.

A 1999 WereNet terminology page
defined "Shift" broadly as:

verb: to change
noun: a change

The same source described Mental Shift
as a change in mindset
from human to animal
or from animal to human.

It also documented multiple forms of shifting,
including:

- Astral Shift,
- Aural Shift,
- Bilocation Shift,
- Dream Shift,
- Mental Shift,
- Phantom Shift,
- Physical Shift,
- Relocation Shift,
- Shadow Shift,
- and Spirit Shift.

The author of that 1999 terminology page
explicitly stated that the definitions
were based on personal experience and research
and should not be treated
as absolute or unquestionable truth.

Current Therian Wiki historical material
also points to a 1993 AHWW discussion
describing a "mental transformation"
in which an individual's thoughts
became increasingly animalistic.

These records do not currently support
a simple historical model in which
early Shifting exclusively meant
an animal-default individual
shifting into a human state.

Status:

HISTORICAL EVIDENCE FOUND —
EARLIER AUTHOR HISTORY CLAIM REQUIRES CORRECTION


### Therian Guide Usage

Therian Guide defines Shifting broadly
as perceived changes
in mental state,
astral or aural state,
or other experience
from human toward animal.

Its examples include:

- Mental Shift,
- Phantom Shift,
- Dream Shift,
- Cameo Shift,
- Astral Shift,
- Sensory Shift,
- Bi-location Shift,
- Aura Shift,
- and Physical Shift.

Therian Guide also distinguishes:

- shifting Therians,
- nonshifting Therians,
- Contherians,
- and Suntherians / Vacillants.

The source presents shifting
as one possible way
Therian experience may manifest,
not as a universal requirement.


### Therian Wiki Usage

Current Therian Wiki describes Shifting
as an experience in which a Therian
feels more like their Theriotype.

It states that shifts may:

- vary in strength and frequency,
- be temporary or persistent,
- occur in multiple forms simultaneously,
- be positive or negative,
- and occur voluntarily or involuntarily.

It also explicitly states that:

- not all Therians shift,
- shifting is not required for Therian identity,
- non-Therians may have shift-like experiences,
- and shifting should not be used by itself
  to determine whether someone is Therian.

Current Therian Wiki partially separates
different dimensions of shifting
by distinguishing:

- how a shift begins,
- shifts that may occur in several modalities,
- and shifts described as specific experiential forms.

This is more structured
than treating every Shift term
as a completely equivalent category.


### SheilaGrace Source Wording

The 2024 source material described
an older and newer interpretation of Shifting.

The earlier author model claimed:

early AHWW usage
→ animal state treated as default
→ shifting into human presentation

modern usage
→ human state treated as default
→ animal characteristics emerge

The source considered
the earlier interpretation
closer to the "essence" of Therianthropy.

It also described Mental Shift
and Phantom Shift
as the most representative forms
and stated that shifting may vary
in type,
frequency,
strength,
and voluntariness.

The source additionally connected
Rage Shift,
loss of control,
clinical lycanthropy,
community support,
and mentor responsibility
to the broader discussion of Shifting.


### Author Expansion / Interpretation

The historical distinction between:

"animal-default → human"

and

"human-default → animal"

appears to have been
an author-level interpretive model.

The currently examined historical sources
do not support treating this
as an established chronological change
in community definition.

The statement that
the earlier model is closer
to the "essence" of Therianthropy
is also an author value judgment,
not a historical fact.

The 2024 material additionally mixed:

- community terminology,
- autobiographical interpretation,
- support guidance,
- clinical speculation,
- and community governance concerns.

These should be separated
in future canonical material.

A further source of clinical framing
was SheilaGrace's information environment
before discovering Therianthropy.

Before encountering Therian terminology,
the author had already encountered
Japanese-language first-person material
framed in terms of "lycanthropy"
or "clinical lycanthropy."

This material had substantial influence
because it provided one of the few
accessible conceptual reference points
through which the author could compare
her own non-human self-experience.

As a result,
clinical lycanthropy terminology
became entangled with later
Therianthropy interpretation.

Current analysis should distinguish:

- the historical influence of that material,
- the author's autobiographical interpretation,
- contemporary Therian terminology,
- and the narrower psychiatric use
  of Clinical Lycanthropy.

The earlier clinical framing
should therefore be preserved
as provenance,
not retained as an assumed causal relationship
between Shifting and psychiatric disorder.


### Personal / Field Observation

SheilaGrace reports
that Japanese-speaking Therians
frequently asked for help
classifying experiences
that they believed might be Shifts.

A recurring pattern was:

experience occurs
→ available glossary is consulted
→ no category feels clearly applicable
→ individual asks an experienced community member
  to interpret or classify the experience

This created substantial demand
for concrete examples
and individualized explanation.

The author consequently expanded
some definitions beyond
the wording of existing reference material
in an attempt to make them practically usable.

This field observation suggests
that insufficient reference detail
can increase mentor workload
and may also encourage
progressive expansion of terminology.

Status:

AUTHOR FIELD OBSERVATION —
ANECDOTAL / REFERENCE-SERVICE CONTEXT


### Cultural Interpretation

Shifting terminology
developed primarily through
English-language community sources.

Experiences described
through other cultural frameworks
may not map cleanly
onto existing Shift terminology.

Japanese folkloric,
religious,
or spiritual interpretations
should therefore not automatically
be translated into
the nearest English-language Shift term.

Where no clear correspondence exists,
the original experience
and cultural interpretation
should be preserved separately
from the community classification.


### Empirical / Clinical Claims

The historical and community sources
examined here establish
the existence and use
of Shifting terminology.

They do not by themselves establish
a clinical,
neurological,
or other scientific mechanism
for every experience classified as a Shift.

Community terminology
should not be presented
as a clinical diagnostic system.

Claims connecting specific Shift types
to:

- neurological mechanisms,
- psychiatric disorders,
- trauma responses,
- developmental mechanisms,
- or treatment outcomes

require independent empirical evidence.

In particular,
the earlier source claim
that uncontrolled Rage Shift
may lead to Clinical Lycanthropy
should not be preserved
as an established clinical relationship
without evidence.


### Spiritual / Metaphysical Claims

Some historical and current Shift terminology
is explicitly based on
spiritual or metaphysical frameworks.

Examples include:

- Astral Shift,
- Aura / Aural Shift,
- Spirit Shift,
- and some forms of Bilocation terminology.

The existence of these community terms
documents spiritual interpretations
within parts of the community.

It does not establish
the underlying metaphysical claims
as empirical facts.

Spiritual interpretations
may be preserved
as interpretations
without being imposed
on individuals using
psychological,
neurological,
cultural,
or unresolved explanatory frameworks.


### Classification Problems

"Shift" terminology does not describe
a single uniform classification axis.

Different terms may refer to:

- phenomenology
  such as Mental or Phantom Shift,

- context or state
  such as Dream Shift,

- referent relationship
  such as Cameo Shift,

- voluntariness
  such as Voluntary / Involuntary Shift,

- trigger
  such as Emotional or Auditory Shift,

- or metaphysical interpretation
  such as Astral or Aura Shift.

Treating all of these
as equivalent sibling categories
can create unnecessary complexity.

Current Therian Wiki partially addresses this
by grouping some terms
according to how they begin
or how they manifest,
but substantial overlap remains.

A future reference system
may therefore benefit
from describing experiences
through multiple dimensions
rather than requiring
one precise subtype label.


### Problems / Risks

- Shifting should not be treated
  as mandatory evidence of Therian identity.

- Absence of Shifting
  should not invalidate Therian identity.

- A Shift-like experience
  should not independently prove
  that someone is Therian.

- Historical community terminology
  should not be treated
  as a scientifically validated taxonomy.

- Classification uncertainty
  should not invalidate
  the reported experience.

- New terminology should not be created
  merely because an experience
  does not fit one existing category perfectly.

- Mentor interpretation
  should not automatically become
  a new community definition.

- Trigger,
  phenomenology,
  referent,
  voluntariness,
  and cultural interpretation
  should not be collapsed
  into one classification axis.

- Historical claims
  should not be inferred
  solely from later community summaries.


### Current Canonical Candidate

Shifting is a community term
for a change or fluctuation
in subjective experience
through which an individual
feels,
thinks,
perceives,
or otherwise experiences themselves
as more closely aligned
with a Theriotype,
Kintype,
or another non-human referent.

Shifting may involve
mental state,
body perception,
dream experience,
self-image,
or other forms of subjective experience.

A Shift may be:

- temporary or persistent,
- voluntary or involuntary,
- mild or intense,
- and composed of more than one experiential dimension.

Shifting is not required
for Therian or Otherkin identity,
and a Shift-like experience
does not independently establish
such an identity.

A reported experience
does not need
to be assigned
to a precise Shift subtype
in order to be meaningful or discussable.

When classification remains uncertain:

preserve the experience first,
and leave the label unresolved.

## TERM: Mental Shift

### Historical Evidence

Experiences recognizable as Mental Shifting
were discussed in the early online were / therian community
during the 1990s.

A 1999 WereNet terminology page
explicitly defined Mental Shift
as a change in mindset
between human and animal states.

That source stated that,
in most cases,
the individual was generally
in a human mindset,
while the shift involved movement
from human to animal
or from animal to human.

The same source framed this
through the spiritual language
of its period,
describing one species
within a person's spirit or soul
as becoming dominant.

The author explicitly warned
that the terminology page
was based on personal experience and research
and should not be treated
as absolute truth.

Current Therian Wiki states
that the term "Mental Shift"
first appeared on
alt.horror.werewolves in 1995
and that similar experiences
were probably discussed earlier.

The primary 1995 use
has not yet been independently located
during this audit.

Status:

HISTORICAL TERM VERIFIED BY 1999 /
1995 ORIGIN CLAIM SECONDARILY DOCUMENTED /
PRIMARY 1995 SOURCE NOT YET VERIFIED


### Therian Guide Usage

Therian Guide describes Mental Shifting
as a change in which
a person's mind becomes more animal-like,
causing them to feel
and react more like the animal.

Examples given by the source include
animal-associated vocalizations
such as yipping,
whining,
or howling.

The Guide also states
that sensory experiences may feel heightened
during Mental Shifts.

Elsewhere on the same page,
the Guide describes different degrees
of Mental Shifting,
ranging from partial
to stronger experiences.

Some of these descriptions,
such as thinking in emotion
rather than sentences
during a "full" shift,
are presented through
community or author experience
rather than empirical evidence.

The Guide also allows
for voluntary and involuntary shifts
and discusses meditation
as one possible way
some individuals attempt
to induce shifting.


### Therian Wiki Usage

Current Therian Wiki describes Mental Shift
as a change in mindset
in which a Therian
thinks and acts more like their Theriotype.

Its dedicated Mental Shift page
describes the experience
as generally temporary
and states that:

Mental Shifts may vary in intensity,
may occur voluntarily or involuntarily,
and may occur during
different emotional states.

The Wiki also documents
Cameo Mental Shifts,
in which the experienced animal referent
is not the individual's known Theriotype.

The current general Shift page
places Mental Shift
among experiential forms of shifting
rather than treating
voluntariness,
trigger,
and intensity
as separate Mental Shift subtypes.


### SheilaGrace Source Wording

The 2024 source describes Mental Shift
as a transition
into an "animalistic state"
in which the individual
perceives themselves
as closer to their Theriotype.

Possible experiences described include:

- animal-like thought processes,
- animal-like emotions,
- urges to vocalize,
- urges to bite or perform other animal-associated behavior,
- variation between voluntary and involuntary experiences,
- and different levels of intensity.

The source places strong emphasis
on controlling Mental Shifts
when animal-associated impulses
could create difficulties
in human social environments.

It also states that:

Suntherians and Vacillant Otherkin
are constantly Mental Shifted,

Shifters may be able
to turn Mental Shift completely off,

Fight-or-Flight responses
may trigger Theriotype-associated
problem-solving behavior,

Rage Shift may emerge
from defensive responses,

and chronic stress
may increase animal-like vigilance.

The source further provides
species-specific examples,
including:

canine pack behavior,
fox territorial behavior,
and emotionally significant flight experiences
among some dragon Otherkin.

It proposes several coping
and identity-expression strategies
for Species Dysphoria
and for unmet animal-associated impulses.


### Author Expansion / Interpretation

The core description
of Mental Shift
as a change or fluctuation in mindset
is consistent
with community terminology.

However,
the 2024 source expands substantially
beyond that core definition.

The following elements
should currently be treated
as author interpretation,
field observation,
or unverified explanatory models:

Fight-or-Flight responses
producing Theriotype-specific behavior,

Rage Shift developing
from those responses,

species-specific personality patterns,

universal stress
from suppressing animal-associated impulses,

and specific coping methods
as generally effective interventions.

The earlier description of Suntherians
as being in
a "constant Mental Shift"
also requires correction.

Historical and current sources
instead describe Suntherian experience
as an integration
of the therioside
into baseline personality,
with fluctuations
rather than discrete Mental Shifts
into the base Theriotype.

Similarly,
the claim that ordinary Shifters
can necessarily turn
Mental Shift completely off
is too categorical
for canonical use.

The statement that
Psychological Therianthropy
has conducted increasingly advanced research
specifically into Mental Shift mechanisms
has not yet been substantiated
during this audit.

Academic research located so far
addresses Therian identity,
self-understanding,
wellbeing,
and related psychological variables,
but does not establish
a specific scientific mechanism
for Mental Shifting.


### Personal / Field Observation

SheilaGrace's source contains
a large number of concrete behavioral examples
and support strategies.

These appear to reflect
a mixture of:

personal experience,
community observation,
support experience,
and attempts to translate
abstract terminology
into practical examples.

Such material may be valuable
as field testimony
and reference-service scaffolding.

However,
the current source
does not consistently identify
which examples came from:

the author's own experience,
individual community reports,
repeated community patterns,
or external research.

Future preservation
should therefore retain these examples
while separating provenance
where it can still be recovered.

Species-specific examples
should not automatically
be generalized
to all individuals
with the same Theriotype.


### Cultural Interpretation

Mental Shift
is comparatively less dependent
on explicitly spiritual terminology
than several other Shift categories.

However,
what counts as
"animal-like,"
"normal human behavior,"
or socially acceptable expression
may vary between individuals
and cultural contexts.

A behavioral example
should therefore not automatically
be treated
as a universal feature
of a species or Theriotype.

The individual's own description
of the experience
should take priority
over forcing behavior
into a culturally predetermined
model of an animal.


### Empirical / Clinical Claims

Mental Shift
is a community phenomenological term.

The existence of the term
and reports of the experience
do not by themselves establish
a neurological,
psychiatric,
or evolutionary mechanism.

Fight-or-Flight
is a recognized human stress response,
but the claim that it specifically activates
Theriotype-dependent instincts
has not been established
by the sources currently examined.

Likewise,
the current audit has not established
that suppressing animal-associated impulses
universally causes psychological harm.

Individual distress
related to suppression,
social masking,
Species Dysphoria,
or lack of safe expression
may still be important
to document and support
without turning the relationship
into a universal causal rule.

Existing academic work located
during this audit
has examined Therian identity
and psychological wellbeing.

It does not,
by itself,
validate the specific mechanisms
proposed in the 2024 Mental Shift description.

Mental Shift
should not be conflated
with Clinical Lycanthropy
or treated as evidence
of psychiatric disorder.


### Spiritual / Metaphysical Claims

Historical Mental Shift terminology
sometimes used spiritual frameworks.

The 1999 WereNet source,
for example,
described Mental Shift
in terms of one species
within a person's spirit or soul
becoming dominant.

This documents
a historical spiritual interpretation.

It should not be converted
into a universal explanation
of Mental Shift.

Individuals may understand
Mental Shifting through:

spiritual,
psychological,
identity-based,
mixed,
or unresolved frameworks.

The canonical definition
does not need
to determine which interpretation
is metaphysically correct.


### Classification Problems

Mental Shift primarily describes
a change in mindset.

Other labels may describe
different dimensions
of the same event.

For example:

Emotional Shift
may describe a trigger,

Voluntary or Involuntary Shift
may describe agency,

Cameo Shift
may describe the experienced referent,

and Berserker / Rage-like terminology
may describe intensity,
emotion,
or behavioral consequences.

These dimensions
can overlap with Mental Shift
without necessarily constituting
separate mutually exclusive phenomena.

A single experience
may therefore be described
more clearly through multiple dimensions
than through one increasingly specific label.


### Problems / Risks

Mental Shift terminology
should not imply that:

animal-associated behavior
is required,

outward vocalization
is required,

an individual loses moral responsibility,

a person literally acquires
an animal nervous system,

specific animal species
have fixed human personality traits,

or intense emotion
automatically proves
that a Mental Shift occurred.

"Control" should not automatically mean
suppression of identity.

Where self-regulation is needed,
the goal should be
safety,
agency,
and workable expression,
rather than elimination
of non-human identity experience.

Conversely,
Therian identity
should not be used
as an automatic justification
for behavior
that harms the individual
or other people.

Support should distinguish:

the validity of the individual's experience

from

the safety and consequences
of a particular action.


### Current Canonical Candidate

Mental Shift
is a community term
for a change or fluctuation
in mindset
during which an individual
experiences their thoughts,
emotions,
urges,
reactions,
or sense of self
as more closely aligned
with a Theriotype,
Kintype,
or other non-human referent.

Mental Shifts may vary
in intensity,
duration,
frequency,
and voluntariness.

They may include
animal-associated thoughts,
emotions,
urges,
or behavior,
but no particular outward behavior
is required.

A Mental Shift
does not imply
physical transformation,
loss of reality testing,
loss of responsibility,
or psychiatric disorder.

Triggers,
intensity,
referent,
and voluntariness
may be described separately
rather than treated
as mutually exclusive
Mental Shift subtypes.

Individual experience
should be preserved first.

Explanatory mechanisms
and subtype labels
may remain provisional.
