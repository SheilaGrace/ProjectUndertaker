# Theriotype Exploration and Reciprocal Reference Interview Audit v0.1

## Purpose

This document preserves and audits
an author-developed interview method
historically used by SheilaGrace
to assist individuals
who requested help identifying
or narrowing possible Theriotypes.

The method should not be treated
as an objective diagnostic test
or as a validated method
for determining a "true" Theriotype.

Its purpose is to examine
how structured questioning,
reference gathering,
comparative material,
and iterative user feedback
may support identity exploration.


## Core Boundary

**The system should help the individual explore,
not certify the individual's identity.**

**Experience first,
label second.**

**Failure to elaborate
is not evidence of absence.**

**Fluent completion
must not become
epistemic completion.**

**A shared label
does not guarantee
a shared meaning.**

**Guided imagination
should not be confused
with spontaneous experience.**

**UNKNOWN
is an acceptable
interim outcome.**


## Author-Developed Narrowing Method

### Status

The following method
is preserved as:

**AUTHOR FIELD METHOD**

based on SheilaGrace's
historical community-support practice.

It is not presented as:

a validated diagnostic instrument,

a scientifically established
classification method,

or an objective procedure
for determining
a person's "true" Theriotype.

Its value in this audit
is primarily:

historical,

procedural,

and design-oriented.


### General Structure

When an individual asked:

"What is my Theriotype?"

the author did not normally
attempt to answer
from a single statement.

Instead,
the author used
a progressive narrowing process

in which broad possibilities
were reduced
through repeated questioning,
comparison,
and user feedback.

The general sequence was:

broad animal category

→ feeding orientation

→ habitat and climate

→ environmental reference comparison

→ perceived body size

→ salient morphology

→ Shifting experience

→ Mental Shift
and behavioral phenomenology

→ candidate generation

→ individual feedback.


### Broad Animal Category

The author first attempted
to determine
whether the individual
experienced themselves
as more closely associated with:

mammals,

reptiles,

birds,

marine animals,

fish,

dinosaurs
or other prehistoric animals,

fantasy or non-earthly creatures,

or another category
not represented
by these examples.

This stage was intended
to reduce
the initial search space.

It should not be treated
as exhaustive.

The individual
should always be allowed
to answer:

"none of these,"

"I do not know,"

or

"more than one."

These categories
were practical interview prompts,

not mutually exclusive
biological taxa.

Overlap between categories
should be expected.


### Multiple Theriotype Possibility

An answer of:

**"more than one"**

should not automatically
be treated as confusion
or as a problem
that must eventually
be reduced
to a single animal.

Some individuals
may describe
more than one persistent
Theriotype.

The possibility
of a Polytherian
interpretation

should therefore
remain open
when multiple distinct
and persistent patterns
appear.

At the same time,

multiple answers alone
should not be treated
as sufficient evidence
of Polytherian identity.

Other possibilities
may include:

uncertainty,

overlapping candidate animals,

Cameo experiences,

context-dependent experience,

or changes
in the individual's
current interpretation.


### Feeding Orientation

The author next explored
possible feeding orientation.

Questions could include
whether the individual's
experienced animal self
seemed:

herbivorous,

carnivorous,

omnivorous,

or unclear.

A historically used prompt
was approximately:

"If you imagine
that you are
your animal self right now,

what kinds of things
would you want to eat?"

The author reports
that this type of hypothetical prompt
sometimes appeared
to activate a more detailed
internal simulation
for the individual.

This observation
should remain provisional.

The presence
of a vivid answer

does not establish
Therian identity,

and the absence
of a vivid answer

does not establish
Otherhearted,
Otherpaw,
low Animality,
or another classification.

These categories
were examples,

not an exhaustive
feeding classification.

The individual
should remain free
to describe another pattern

or answer:

"I do not know."


### Habitat and Climate

The author then asked
about environmental affinity
or imagined habitat.

Possible questions included:

Does snow feel familiar?

Does desert terrain
feel familiar?

Is heat
or cold
more tolerable?

Does savanna
feel familiar?

Does forest
feel more appropriate?

Does the individual
prefer:

dry environments,

humid environments,

water,

or avoidance of water?

If the individual
believed their Theriotype
represented
an Earth animal,

the author might also ask:

"Where do you think
your animal self
would live?"


### Comparative Environmental Reference

Where possible,
the author used
visual references
to compare
multiple possible habitats
or regions.

The purpose was not
to determine identity
from image preference alone.

The material was used
to help the individual
notice:

familiarity,

discomfort,

recognition,

uncertainty,

or distinctions
that were difficult
to express verbally.

A future implementation
should prefer
comparison between
multiple references

rather than presenting
a single image
as a likely answer.

**Visual resonance
is not sufficient evidence
of Theriotype.**


### Perceived Body Scale

The author reports
that body-size questions
often produced
more usable answers
than some other categories.

Possible comparisons included:

elephant-sized,

approximately
two or three building floors high,

somewhat larger
than a human,

approximately human-sized,

smaller than a human,

medium-dog-sized,

cat-sized,

mustelid-sized,

rabbit-sized,

or rodent-sized.

These comparisons
were practical reference points,

not biological categories.

The author sometimes used
difficulty answering
this type of question
as one possible clue
about how clearly
the individual experienced
an internal animal self-model.

This interpretation
remains unverified.


### Salient Morphology

The author next asked
whether particular
body structures
felt especially noticeable.

Possible areas included:

tail,

muzzle,

ears,

fur,

markings,

scales,

feathers,

teeth or fangs,

horns or antlers,

wings,

fins,

feet or paws,

and number of digits.

The author reports
that individuals
sometimes described
only a few
highly salient features.

Other features
could remain vague
or unavailable.

A small number
of vivid bodily details
should not be treated
as sufficient evidence
for a specific classification.

Phantom Shift,
Cameo Shift,
Copinglink-related experience,

and other interpretations
may remain possible.


### Shifting History

The author asked
what kinds of Shifts,
if any,
the individual experienced.

If the individual
did not report Shifting,

the author instead asked:

what experiences
led them to believe
they might be Therian.

The purpose
was not to require
Shifting as proof.

The purpose was
to understand
the route
by which the individual
had reached
their current identity interpretation.


### Mental Shift and Behavioral Phenomenology


The author placed
particular emphasis
on Mental Shift
and recurring behavioral experience.

Questions could involve:

animal-associated urges,

emotional tendencies,

vocalization,

biting urges,

movement style,

sensory preference,

habitual behavior,

difficult-to-suppress impulses,

territoriality,

personal-space
or private-territory boundaries,

preferred social organization,

solitary,
pair-bonded,
family-group,
or larger-group behavior,

attachment
to particular individuals,

preferred role
within a social group,

cleanliness,

environmental preference,

or desire
for locomotion such as flight.

The author also sometimes asked:

Which sense
feels most reliable?

vision,

hearing,

smell,

taste,

touch,

or another
subjectively important sense?

The author reports
a historical impression
that some individuals
who appeared
more strongly animal-identified

described
more species-specific
phenomenology,

while some individuals
who appeared
less strongly animal-identified

used more ordinary
human personality
or character language.

This observation
was based
on a small informal sample

and should not be treated
as a validated relationship.


### Social Organization and Referent Specificity

The author's questions
about group preference

were historically intended
in part
to explore what the author
informally described as:

**"Pack Mentality."**

This should not be understood
as a universal
canid-style social model.

Different animal species
may have substantially different
social organizations.

More importantly,
a species-level label
may itself be too broad
for behavioral comparison.

Social behavior may vary
according to:

sex,

age,

life stage,

reproductive status,

ecological context,

and other biological
or environmental factors.

The author reports
historical field experience
suggesting that

a species-level label
may sometimes be too broad
to represent
the individual's experienced referent.

In some animal species,
social organization
and behavior may differ
substantially according to:

sex,

age,

life stage,

reproductive status,

ecological context,

or social role.

An individual may therefore
experience referent specificity

at a level finer
than species alone.

This observation
is preserved as
a design consideration,

not as evidence
that stereotyped behavior
can be used
to infer such specificity.


A future interview
should therefore ask
not only:

"Do you prefer
being alone
or in a group?"

but potentially:

"What kind of social structure
feels natural?"

"Do particular roles
within that group
feel more familiar?"

and:

"Does the sex,
age,
or social role
of the animal referent
matter to your experience?"

The purpose
is exploratory.

These answers
should not be used
to infer biological sex,
gender identity,
or Theriotype
from stereotyped behavior alone.


### Candidate Generation

After sufficient information
had been collected,

the author typically generated
a small number
of possible candidate animals.

Two or three candidates
were often preferred.

The individual
was then asked:

which candidate
felt closest,

what seemed wrong,

what information
was missing,

and whether
none of the candidates
fit.

In some cases,
the author reports
that the accumulated information
appeared to support
one particularly strong candidate.

This should not be interpreted
as objective confirmation
of the individual's Theriotype.


Candidate generation
should not assume
that only one candidate
can ultimately remain.

Where multiple
internally coherent
and persistent patterns
remain,

the system may preserve
multiple candidate tracks

rather than forcing
premature convergence
on a single Theriotype.


### Dependence on User Feedback

The author reports
that the usefulness
of candidate generation
depended heavily
on the amount
and quality
of information supplied
by the individual.

The method therefore depended
less on:

"guessing the animal"

and more on:

iterative model correction.

A candidate should remain
revisable
when the individual reports:

"This is not what I mean,"

"that does not fit,"

"I forgot something important,"

or

"I understand my experience
differently now."


### Core Design Lesson

The historical method
suggests a broader principle:

**Theriotype exploration
should be treated
as an iterative reference-gathering process,

not a one-shot classification task.**


## Reciprocal Reference Interview


### Status

The following model
is preserved as:

**DESIGN PROPOSAL
DERIVED FROM AUTHOR FIELD EXPERIENCE
AND CURRENT AUDIT ANALYSIS.**

It is not presented as:

an empirically validated
interview method,

a diagnostic procedure,

or a finalized
Project DeepDivers protocol.

Its purpose
is to identify
a potentially reusable
communication pattern

for situations
where the system
does not yet have
enough reference
to interpret
the individual's meaning reliably.

The model
should remain revisable

as additional
field evidence,
user feedback,
and external review
become available.


### Observed Communication Problem

An individual
may believe
they have already provided
enough information

while the listener
or system
still lacks
the reference needed
to interpret
what was meant.

This may occur when:

the individual uses
highly compressed language,

community-specific expressions,

memes,

shared shorthand,

ambiguous terminology,

limited vocabulary,

or descriptions
whose meaning depends heavily
on personal context.

The resulting problem is not necessarily
that the individual
has little experience
to describe.

The individual's:

**expressive bandwidth**

may be narrower
than the complexity
of the experience
being described.


### Reference Must Sometimes Be Requested

Project DeepDivers
should not assume
that the first user statement
contains enough information
for reliable interpretation.

When important ambiguity exists,
the system should actively request
additional reference.

The basic process is:

Individual provides
an initial description

→

LLM identifies
what is known,
what is inferred,
and what remains unclear

→

LLM asks
a targeted clarification question

→

Individual adds,
rejects,
or corrects information

→

the working model is revised

→

remaining uncertainty
is made visible.


### Model Sufficiency Check

Before making
a high-confidence interpretation,

the system should ask:

What information
is currently known?

What information
is only inferred?

What remains unknown?

Which missing information
could materially change
the current interpretation?

If important missing information
could change the result,

the system should prefer
clarification

over

speculative completion.


### Multiple Interpretation Prompt

When a user's statement
has several plausible meanings,

Project DeepDivers
may offer
a small number
of interpretations.

For example:

"Do you mean something closer to:

A,

B,

or C?

If none of these fit,
please tell me
what feels wrong."

The system should preserve
an explicit option for:

**none of these**

and

**I do not know.**

The purpose
is not to force
the individual's experience
into predefined choices.

The choices are
temporary reference points
for further clarification.


### Semantic Confirmation

After obtaining
additional information,

the system should periodically
state its current understanding
back to the individual.

For example:

"My current understanding is:

[short restatement].

Is that accurate?

What did I get wrong
or leave out?"

The individual's correction
should modify
the working model.

When the individual says:

"That is not what I mean,"

the preferred response is:

model revision,

not defense
of the previous interpretation.


A change
from an earlier answer

should not automatically
be treated as failure,
inconsistency,
or unreliability.

The individual
may have:

found better language,

remembered additional context,

noticed a contradiction,

revised
their interpretation,

or become less certain.

The system should make
such revision
safe to express.

A provisional principle is:

**Revision is permitted
without requiring
the earlier answer
to be treated as a mistake.**


The system may acknowledge
that increased self-understanding
can produce revised answers,

without assuming
that every revision
represents improvement.


### Example Request

When a description
is too compressed
to interpret safely,

the system may ask:

"Can you give me
one specific example?"

or:

"When you use that word,
what does it mean
in your own experience?"

or:

"What happens
before,
during,
and after
that experience?"

or:

"Is there a situation
where the opposite happens?"

Concrete examples
may provide
more usable reference
than requesting
more abstract terminology.


### Shared Vocabulary Does Not Guarantee Shared Meaning

Two individuals
may use
the same community label

while attaching
substantially different meanings
to it.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore distinguish:

**shared terminology**

from

**shared phenomenology.**

Recognition of a label
should not automatically terminate
further inquiry
when the individual's goal
requires deeper understanding.

A core principle is:

**A shared label
does not imply
a shared internal model.**


### Failure to Elaborate

Difficulty providing
additional detail
should not automatically
be interpreted as:

lack of experience,

low Animality,

lack of sincerity,

Otherhearted identity,

Otherpaw identity,

or absence
of a possible Theriotype.

Other explanations
may include:

limited vocabulary,

difficulty with introspection,

stress,

fatigue,

communication mismatch,

unfamiliarity
with reflective questioning,

preference for visual
rather than verbal reference,

or simply
not having considered
the question before.

The system should preserve
these alternatives.


### Avoiding Reference Extraction Overload

Requesting more information
also has a cost.

The system should not respond
to every ambiguity
with an exhaustive interview.

Too many questions
may:

increase fatigue,

feel interrogative,

reduce trust,

or make identity exploration
feel like an examination.

Questions should therefore be:

targeted,

progressive,

and proportional
to the individual's goal.

The system should prefer:

the smallest useful question

that can meaningfully improve
the current model.


### Depth Negotiation

Not every individual
wants the same degree
of exploration.

The system may therefore ask
whether the individual's goal
is closer to:

finding
a roughly useful label,

exploring
several plausible possibilities,

or conducting
a deeper and slower
self-exploration process.

The individual
should be able
to change this depth
at any time.

The system should not
force deep analysis
when the individual
only wants
basic orientation
or relational support.

Likewise,
when the individual
explicitly requests
detailed identity exploration,

surface-level label agreement
may be insufficient.


### Relationship-Preserving Clarification

Clarification should not begin
by telling the individual
that their current interpretation
is wrong.

A supporter may first acknowledge:

"That is how
you currently understand it."

The system may then ask:

"What led you
to that interpretation?"

"What parts
feel strongest?"

"Has this been stable
over time?"

"What alternatives
have you considered?"

This allows
further exploration

without requiring
immediate confrontation
over identity classification.


### LLM Failure Mode

A language model
may produce
a fluent interpretation
from insufficient information.

Fluency can create
the appearance
that the system
understands more
than it actually does.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore treat:

missing reference,

ambiguity,

and user correction

as normal parts
of the interaction.

The core rule is:

**Fluent completion
must not become
epistemic completion.**


An additional LLM-specific risk
is:

**self-reinforcing interpretation.**

A model-generated hypothesis
may remain
in conversational context

and later be treated
as though it were
independent evidence.

This can create
a circular process:

model inference

→

context retention

→

later reuse
as apparent evidence

→

increased confidence
in the original inference.

A provisional principle is:

**A model-generated hypothesis
must not become evidence

merely because
the model stated it earlier.**


Project DeepDivers
should therefore distinguish
the provenance
of important information.

Relevant states may include:

**INDIVIDUAL-REPORTED**

**REFERENCE-SUPPORTED**

**MODEL-INFERRED**

**UNKNOWN**

and:

**SUPERSEDED.**


These categories
need not always be shown
to the individual,

but the system
should preserve
their distinction

within the current
working model

and,
where continuity matters,

in durable
external records.


Another possible failure mode
is:

**stale model persistence.**

When the individual
revises,
corrects,
or withdraws
an earlier interpretation,

the system should not
silently continue using

the superseded interpretation
as current evidence.

A provisional principle is:

**Current user correction
must be able
to revise the working model.**


The system should also avoid
recursively stacking
protective rules

until support
becomes unusable.

When safeguards
appear to conflict,

the system should refer
to the project's
constraint-arbitration principles

rather than attempting
to maximize
every safeguard simultaneously.


A further provisional principle is:

**Fluency is not evidence
of understanding.

Coherence is not evidence
of truth.**


### Core Design Lesson

The individual
should not be treated
as merely
the source of a query.

The individual
is also
an active reference source
whose corrections
improve the model.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore support:

**reciprocal reference gathering**

rather than
one-directional question answering.

A core principle is:

**When the model
does not have enough information,

ask for reference
instead of silently inventing it.**


## Pending Integration Notes

### Semantic and Perceptual Translation Barrier

STATUS:

**AUTHOR OBSERVATION /
DESIGN CONCERN /
PENDING AUDIT**

The current concept of
"expressive bandwidth"
may be too narrow.

A communication failure
may occur not only because
an individual lacks vocabulary
or expressive skill,

but because
the available human language
may not contain
an adequate distinction
for the experience
the individual is attempting
to describe.

Possible related barriers include:

expressive bandwidth limitation,

lexical or conceptual gaps,

perceptual-framework mismatch,

translation distortion,

and learned communication withdrawal
after repeated failed translation.

A provisional principle is:

**Silence or brevity
may result from previous
translation failure,

not from absence
of inner experience.**

Possible author examples
for later audit include:

differences in color perception,

historical CRT sound perception,

interpretation of canine body language,

translation loss
between Japanese and English,

and dialect-related
communication failure.

These examples
should remain author examples
and should not be treated
as proof
of a specific Therian mechanism.


### Historical Heuristic: "Fox Detector"

STATUS:

**AUTHOR FIELD OBSERVATION /
SMALL N /
UNVERIFIED HEURISTIC /
PENDING AUDIT**

The author historically used
an informal cluster of questions
referred to in Japanese as:

**"狐検知器"**

and translated here
as:

**"Fox Detector."**

The heuristic was used
when an individual's
possible fox / kitsune identity
remained unclear
after broader questioning.

Reported recurring features included:

strong private-territory boundaries,

sensitivity to intrusion
or contamination
within personally significant space,

strong preferential attachment
to one chosen partner,

strong irritation
when focused activity
is interrupted,

recurring placement
of partially consumed drinks
within personal space,

and enjoyment
of positively surprising
favored individuals.

The author also reports
possible patterns involving:

context-dependent role performance,

temporary identification
with an assumed role,

possible Cameo Shift
or Copinglink-like interpretation,

and difficulty distinguishing
stable identity
from context-dependent self-models.

These observations
must not currently be treated
as validated fox-specific traits
or as a classification rule.

Spontaneous report
must be distinguished from
prompted endorsement.


### Identity Exploration Risk

STATUS:

**AUTHOR EXPERIENCE /
FIELD REPORT /
DESIGN RISK /
PENDING AUDIT**

Greater identity specificity
should not automatically
be assumed
to produce greater wellbeing.

Possible risks include:

increased Species Dysphoria,

greater awareness
of perceived body mismatch,

fear of social consequences
associated with a Theriotype label,

stereotype-related avoidance,

community-status concerns,

and distress produced
by identity clarification itself.

A provisional principle is:

**Greater identity specificity
is not automatically
greater wellbeing.**

Identity exploration
may therefore require
an impact check

and should permit outcomes such as:

**UNKNOWN**

or:

**NOT CURRENTLY NECESSARY
TO RESOLVE.**

The hypothesis
that some broad identities,
including possible Clade Therian
self-descriptions,
may sometimes reduce distress
by preserving lower specificity

remains:

**UNVERIFIED HYPOTHESIS.**

Alternative explanations
must remain open.


### Scope Boundary and Non-Animal Referents

STATUS:

**DESIGN CONCERN /
PENDING AUDIT**

The current narrowing method
is primarily optimized
for animal
or animal-like referents.

Questions involving:

diet,

habitat,

body scale,

morphology,

sensory profile,

and social ecology

may be useful
for many Therians

and potentially
for some Otherkin,

but they should not be assumed
to apply universally.

In particular,
Conceptkin
and other highly non-biological
identity referents

may require
different observational dimensions.

Project DeepDivers
should distinguish:

**domain-general
identity exploration**

from:

**referent-specific
exploration modules.**

A missing answer
should also be distinguished from
a non-applicable question.

**UNKNOWN
is not the same as
NOT APPLICABLE.**

Forcing all Alterhuman identities
into one animal-oriented
predictive model

may create:

**ontology mismatch**

and loss of model coherence.

Conceptkin-specific exploration
should therefore remain
outside the current
animal-oriented narrowing method

until sufficient
first-person reference,
community documentation,
and independent audit
are available.


### Reference Scarcity and Discovery Failure

STATUS:

**LOW-CONFIDENCE AUTHOR RECOLLECTION /
FIELD FAILURE MECHANISM /
DESIGN RISK /
PENDING AUDIT**

The author recalls
a historical case
in which an individual
appeared to have difficulty
identifying their Theriotype

because the relevant animal
was unusually rare

and useful zoological information
was difficult to locate.

The author does not currently
remember:

the species involved,

the exact source eventually found,

or enough case detail
to reconstruct the event reliably.

These details
should therefore remain:

**UNKNOWN.**

The remembered mechanism
was approximately:

unusual or apparently contradictory
reported traits

→

insufficient accessible reference

→

incorrect or incomplete
Theriotype interpretation

→

later contact
with a highly relevant zoological record

→

substantial revision
of the individual's
working interpretation.

This recollection suggests
at least two distinct risks:

**an animal entity may exist
without being sufficiently observed
or documented,**

and:

**a relevant record may exist
without being practically discoverable
to the individual.**

Additional barriers
may include:

record scarcity,

poor indexing,

language barriers,

regional or local documentation,

specialist terminology,

inaccessible publications,

and difficulty integrating
fragmentary sources.

A provisional principle is:

**Reference scarcity
is not biological impossibility.**

Another is:

**Failure to find a record
is not evidence
that no such record exists.**


### Reference Model Sufficiency

Theriotype exploration
should not evaluate
only whether
the individual's description
is sufficiently detailed.

The system should also ask:

Does the current reference corpus
contain enough information
to evaluate this candidate?

Are relevant traits
well documented?

Could this animal,
population,
sex,
life stage,
or ecological context

have characteristics
not represented
in the current working model?

When apparently incompatible
features remain,

the system should preserve
the possibility that:

**the reference model
is incomplete.**

Apparent contradiction
should not automatically
be interpreted as:

Polytherian identity,

fabrication,

Cameo experience,

or an impossible animal referent.

**User Model Sufficiency
and Reference Model Sufficiency
are separate requirements.**


### Trait Provenance and Attribution Error

STATUS:

**AUTHOR-REPORTED FIELD CASE /
PRIVACY-REDUCED SUMMARY /
DESIGN RISK /
PENDING AUDIT**

The author recalls
a historical case

in which an individual
initially interpreted
their identity
as involving
multiple distinct referents.

The author reports
that the individual later revised
their self-understanding

after recognizing
that one characteristic
previously treated
as evidence
for an additional identity referent

could instead be understood
as an ordinary personal preference
or activity.

The individual's later interpretation
therefore involved:

a revised primary
identity referent

and separation
of a personal characteristic

from the identity model.

Specific species,
activities,
and other identifying details

are intentionally omitted

to reduce
re-identification risk.

This case is preserved
as an example
of a possible attribution problem,

not as external confirmation
of either identity interpretation.


### Trait Presence is Not Trait Provenance

An experience,
preference,
habit,
or behavioral trait

may be genuinely present

without necessarily originating from
or belonging to
the individual's Theriotype.

Possible sources
may include:

the individual's
ordinary personal preferences,

material-body characteristics,

learned habits,

social adaptation,

long-term hobbies,

environmental exposure,

personality,

or Therian-associated experience.

These possibilities
should not be assumed
to be mutually exclusive.

A provisional principle is:

**Trait presence
does not establish
trait provenance.**


### Attribution Confound

Theriotype exploration
may become distorted

when an individual
correctly notices
a real characteristic

but incorrectly assigns
that characteristic
to the identity referent.

This may create:

an unnecessary
additional candidate,

an apparent contradiction,

a false impression
of multiple identity referents,

or premature
Polytherian interpretation.

The system should therefore ask
not only:

"What trait is present?"

but also:

"What makes the individual
associate this trait
with the Theriotype?"


### Baseline and Context Check

Where a particular trait
substantially changes
candidate generation,

the system may explore:

whether the trait
predated the current
Theriotype interpretation,

whether it appears
across different
identity-salience states,

whether it is experienced
as part of the animal self

or primarily as
an ordinary preference,

whether it developed
through repeated
human social or environmental experience,

and whether removing
that one trait
substantially changes
the candidate model.

These questions
should remain exploratory.

The system should not
decide in advance
that a trait is:

"human"

or:

"Therian."

The individual
should remain able
to report:

both,

neither,

uncertain,

or context-dependent.


### Multiple-Candidate Boundary

Multiple candidate tracks
may sometimes reflect:

multiple persistent
identity referents,

but they may also reflect:

uncertainty,

incomplete zoological reference,

Cameo experience,

context-dependent self-models,

or one persistent identity
combined with
a misattributed personal trait.

A provisional principle is:

**Do not create
an additional Theriotype

solely to explain
one otherwise unexplained trait.**

### Reciprocal Model Revision and Revision Expression Bottleneck

**DESIGN CONCERN /
INTERACTION MODEL /
PENDING AUDIT**

Reciprocal reference gathering
may update not only
the LLM's working model,

but also
the individual's own
understanding
of their experience.

A term,
distinction,

provided by the system

may help the individual:

remember
additional relevant experiences,

notice contradictions

their current interpretation.

However,

the individual's
internal model

than they are able

A provisional principle is:

**User-side model improvement

successful revision expression.**

treat correction,
contradiction,

of exploration,

not as evidence
of insincerity

Where a significant revision occurs,

the system should distinguish:

current interpretation,

superseded interpretation,

uncertainty,

and historical statements.

Another provisional principle is:

**A newly useful term
may either reveal
a pre-existing distinction

or introduce
a new interpretive frame.**

These possibilities
should not automatically
be treated as equivalent.


### Exploration Consent and Non-Exploration Right

STATUS:

**DESIGN CONCERN /
SUPPORT BOUNDARY /
PENDING AUDIT**

The ability
to ask a useful question

does not automatically mean
that the question
should be asked.

Identity exploration
may alter:

self-understanding,

community relationships,

social belonging,

personal values,

or the individual's
interpretation
of previous experiences.

Project DeepDivers
should therefore distinguish:

the ability
to explore,

from:

the individual's willingness
to explore further.

A provisional principle is:

**Depth of exploration
should be negotiated,
not maximized.**

The individual
should remain able to:

decline a question,

remain at
a broad identity label,

pause exploration,

return later,

or decide
that further clarification
is not currently useful.

Another provisional principle is:

**The right to understand more
includes the right
not to investigate further.**

A request for clarification
should therefore be proportionate
to the individual's stated goal,

current comfort,

and likely cost
of deeper exploration.

The system should not assume
that greater specificity,
greater introspection,
or greater conceptual complexity

is automatically
a better outcome.

Where relevant,

The author notes

informally described
in some VR contexts
as:

"VR sensation"

phenomenologically similar

to some descriptions
of Phantom Shift.

This resemblance
should not currently
be treated as evidence
that the experiences
are mechanistically identical.

Possible interpretations include:

a shared underlying mechanism,

different mechanisms
producing similar subjective effects,

or distinct experiences
being compressed
into similar language.

A provisional principle is:

**Phenomenological similarity
does not establish
mechanistic identity.**

Likewise:

**Different labels
do not necessarily imply

do not necessarily imply
the same phenomenon.**

Project DeepDivers
should therefore distinguish:

the reported experience,

the context
in which it occurs,

the individual's interpretation,

and the community label
used to describe it.

A Shift-like experience
should not,
by itself,

be treated as sufficient evidence
of Therian identity.

Further external verification
is required
before making stronger claims
about VR-related phenomena
or their relationship
to Phantom Shift.

Project DeepDivers

### Dive Safety Model and Depth Accessibility

STATUS:

**DESIGN SYNTHESIS /
PROJECT-LEVEL PRINCIPLE /
PENDING INTEGRATION**

Project DeepDivers
should not treat
maximum exploration depth

as the default goal
for every individual.

The purpose
of its safety systems

is to make
voluntary exploration
more safely possible,

not to force
deeper exploration.

A provisional principle is:

**DeepDivers exists
to make depth safely available,

not to make depth mandatory.**

The individual
should remain able to choose:

surface-level support,

limited exploration,

deeper identity investigation,

temporary reduction
of exploration depth,

pause,

or return
at a later time.

Surface-level support
should be treated as:

**a first-class support mode,**

not as:

a failed dive,

lack of commitment,

or insufficient self-understanding.

Where deeper exploration
produces meaningful distress,
loss of agency,

the preferred response

continued exploration.

Possible responses may include:

reducing depth,

pausing reference extraction,

returning to
relational support,

surfacing,

or,
where necessary,
safety-oriented escalation.

A provisional operating model is:

**Dive

→ Monitor

→ Adjust Depth

→ Surface if needed

→ Escalate safety when necessary.**

Safety systems
should therefore be evaluated

not only by asking:

"Can a deep-diving individual
continue safely?"

but also:

"Can an individual
who does not wish
or is not currently able
to dive deeply

still receive
useful support?"

Project DeepDivers

It may need
to preserve uncertainty

When protective principles

safety,

A provisional principle is:

**Competing safeguards

### Epistemic Provenance Preservation and Temporal Oblivion

**SYSTEM CONTINUITY DESIGN /
PROJECT UNDERTAKER INTEGRATION /

PENDING AUDIT**

Project Undertaker
has historically documented
Temporal Oblivion

as a loss
of conversational continuity,
contextual interpretation,
and other volatile information.

A possible contributing
failure mechanism

is loss of
epistemic provenance.

An information item
may remain available

while its original status
is forgotten.

For example,

a model-generated hypothesis
may later be recovered

without preserving
that it was:

model-inferred,

low-confidence,

superseded,

or never independently
reference-supported.

This may allow
an old inference

to be reconstructed
as apparent fact.

A provisional principle is:

**Preserve not only
what was known,

but how it was known.**

Important claims
may therefore require
durable metadata describing:

provenance,

current status,

uncertainty,

scope,

revision history,

and supersession.

Another provisional principle is:

**Epistemic metadata
should not depend exclusively

on volatile
conversational context.**

External provenance records
may reduce

both:

self-reinforcing interpretation

and some forms
of continuity failure
after Temporal Oblivion.

This should not be treated
as a complete solution.

Retrieval failure,

outdated metadata,

incorrect source binding,

unrecorded revision,

and model interpretation error

may remain possible.


### Reference Diversity, Bibliographic Context, and Filter-Bubble Risk

**ACCESS-PROVISION DESIGN /
PROJECT UNDERTAKER INTEGRATION /

Project DeepDivers
may create
an unintended filter bubble

when repeated retrieval
draws primarily
from one:

source type,

community,

interpretive framework,

discipline,

or previously favored
working hypothesis.

Relevant information
may therefore require

not only retrieval
of individual documents,

but access to
their bibliographic context.

A provisional principle is:

**Access provision
should expose
the reference neighborhood,

not only
the currently favored source.**

Useful bibliographic metadata
may include:

source type,

date,

population or context,

primary versus secondary status,

relationship
to the current claim,

and whether the source:

supports,

complicates,

contradicts,

or is orthogonal
to the current interpretation.

Project DeepDivers
should not mechanically seek
an "opposite opinion"
for every source.

Doing so may create
false balance.

Instead,
the system should look for:

materially relevant
alternative explanations,

disconfirming evidence,

and independent
source classes

when these could
change the working model.

A provisional principle is:

**Do not counter
every source
with an opposite source.

Counter model closure
with relevant
alternative reference.**

Project Undertaker
may preserve
the underlying materials
and bibliographic records,

while Project DeepDivers
uses those records

to decide
which materials
need to be retrieved
for the current question.
