# Therianthropy Terminology Audit v0.1

## Purpose

This document audits Therianthropy-related terminology and claims
before creation of new canonical documents.

Its purpose is to separate:

- historical community usage,
- current community usage,
- author interpretation,
- personal experience,
- empirical or clinical claims,
- speculative claims,
- and candidate canonical definitions.

The source documents must not be silently rewritten.

Historical wording should remain recoverable even when later analysis
finds it inaccurate, incomplete, culturally specific, or outdated.

Newer usage is not automatically more authoritative.

Older usage is not automatically more authoritative.

Claims about community history, terminology, medicine, psychology,
or science require external verification where appropriate.


## TERM: セリアンスロピー / Therianthropy

### Author Context

SheilaGrace experienced strong concern that newer role-play,
fashion-oriented, or social-media-mediated animal identity cultures
could become conflated with Therianthropy.

For the author, this was not merely a terminology dispute.
Therian identity was deeply connected to personal history,
value formation, and Identificational Value.

This concern influenced the stronger boundary-maintaining language
used in the 2024 source material.

Claims about the historical boundaries of the Therian community,
the development of newer terminology, and the influence of social
media require independent external verification.


### Source wording

The 2024 source describes Therianthropy as the phenomenon of
considering or feeling oneself to be a non-human animal,
and compares it to belief, faith, religious outlook, or religion.


### Intended meaning in 2024

The religion analogy was not primarily intended to define
Therianthropy as an institutional religion.

SheilaGrace used "religion" in a broad, Shinto-influenced sense:
a deeply internalized cultural and moral framework that influences
what an individual regards as important, sacred, undesirable,
natural, or self-evidently appropriate.

The analogy arose from a perceived structural similarity between
culturally internalized intuitions such as
"this is naturally how one behaves within this culture"
and species-identity intuitions such as
"this is naturally how a wolf would behave."

For SheilaGrace personally, wolfdog identity strongly influenced
value formation, behavior, Identificational Value, and later
philosophical development.


### Current Author Position

SheilaGrace still considers this analogy personally meaningful,
even while recognizing community positions that distinguish
Therianthropy from religion.

The analogy should therefore be preserved as an individual
interpretive framework, not treated as a universal definition.


### Preserve

- Therian identity can profoundly influence value formation.
- Species identity may function as an interpretive framework.
- Therianthropy may carry importance comparable to culture,
  philosophy, spirituality, or religion for some individuals.
- Personal identity and Identificational Value may strongly interact.


### Problems / Risks

- "Therianthropy is religion" overgeneralizes an individual analogy.
- Religious language may imply doctrine, orthodoxy, believers,
  conversion, or institutional authority that Therianthropy
  does not necessarily possess.
- Species-derived moral intuitions should not be presumed universal
  among all Therians.
- Personal discomfort with newer community cultures must not be
  presented as objective membership criteria.


### Historical / Community Claims Requiring Verification

- Early community definitions of Therianthropy.
- Historical positions separating Therianthropy from religion.
- Evolution of Japanese terminology.
- History and current meaning of Otherpaw.
- Relationship between TikTok-era usage and older community usage.


### Empirical Claims

None of the religion analogy itself should be treated as
an empirical scientific claim.


### Interpretive / Personal Claims

The Shinto comparison and species-based moral framework are
SheilaGrace's interpretive model and autobiographical experience.


### Current Canonical Candidate

Therianthropy refers to a persistent identification with,
or experience of oneself as, a non-human animal.

For some individuals, this identity may also influence values,
self-understanding, spirituality, philosophy, or moral intuition.
These interpretations vary between individuals and should not
be treated as necessary conditions of Therian identity.

PARTIALLY AUDITED — EXTERNAL VERIFICATION REQUIRED


## TERM: セリアン / Therian

### Author Context

SheilaGrace understands Therian identity as a deeply integrated,
generally involuntary animal identity rather than a hobby,
role-play, fashion, or deliberately constructed persona.

A central personal model is the concept of a "Ground":
an animal identity experienced as a foundational point of self
to which the individual repeatedly returns.

SheilaGrace historically used stronger authenticity language such as
"true Therian" in response to perceived conflation between
Therian identity and role-play or socially motivated animal performance.

This concern remains personally significant, but should not by itself
be used as an objective test of another individual's identity.


### Intended Meaning

The intended distinction was not primarily whether a person
shows many visible animal behaviors.

The intended distinction concerned the relationship between
the individual and the animal identity:

- whether the animal is experienced as part of the self,
- whether that identity is experienced as voluntarily chosen,
- whether it functions primarily as identity, connection,
  coping mechanism, or role-play,
- and whether it remains meaningful independent of external reward.

"Motivation" was historically used by SheilaGrace as a major heuristic,
but should not be treated as the sole classification criterion.


### Preserve

- Therian identity should not be reduced to animal role-play.
- Visible behavior does not determine whether someone is Therian.
- Therian identity is commonly described as integral and involuntary.
- Self-exploration may result in changing one's chosen label.
- People exploring whether they are Therian should not be rejected
  merely for uncertainty or an initial misidentification.
- Distinct concepts such as Otherhearted, Copinglink, Otherpaw,
  and Therian should not be collapsed into one category.


### Problems / Risks

- "True Therian" can imply an authority capable of certifying
  another person's internal identity.
- "Innate" is stronger than "involuntary" and requires evidence.
- Motivation alone cannot reliably classify an individual's identity.
- External observers cannot safely infer hidden motives such as
  attention-seeking without evidence.
- Copinglink and Otherhearted should not be reduced respectively
  to negative and positive motivation.
- Depth / Density must not become an authenticity,
  pathology, worth, or treatment-urgency score.


### Epistemic Boundary: Self-Identification and External Verification

As of 2026, there is no established objective test,
biomarker, or diagnostic procedure that can independently certify
whether an individual is a Therian.

Therian identity is therefore primarily accessed through
the individual's own reported experience, longitudinal self-observation,
and interpretation of that experience.

This does not mean that all identity claims are empirically equivalent,
nor does it grant an external observer reliable access
to another person's internal identity.

External observers may evaluate:

- whether a description is consistent with working community definitions,
- whether a person is consciously describing role-play or identity,
- whether factual claims are accurate,
- and whether conduct harms other people.

They should not claim scientific certainty about
another individual's internal Therian identity.

Terms such as "true Therian" may be preserved in historical
or autobiographical records,
but should not be used as if they represented
a scientifically validated category.


### Exploratory Role-play and Simulation

Role-play, virtual embodiment, avatars,
and simulated animal embodiment may be used
as tools for identity exploration.

Using Second Life, VRChat, creative writing,
role-play, visualization, or similar environments
to explore the question
"Does this way of embodiment feel meaningfully like me?"
does not by itself imply that the person is merely role-playing.

At the same time, positive resonance during role-play
does not constitute proof of Therian identity.

Simulation should be treated as one exploratory method
among several, alongside longitudinal self-observation,
research, journaling, reflection on spontaneous experiences,
and comparison of experiences across different contexts.

Exploration is not fraud.

Uncertainty is not fraud.

A person may explore a Therian interpretation
and later conclude that another concept describes them better.


### SheilaGrace Framework: Ground

"Ground" is SheilaGrace's phenomenological model for an animal identity
experienced as foundational rather than instrumentally adopted.

It may be useful for introspection,
but is not currently an externally validated diagnostic criterion.


### SheilaGrace Framework: Depth / Density

Depth / Density is a descriptive framework intended to observe
variation in the salience or intensity of animality among individuals.

It must not be interpreted as:

- a measure of whether someone is a "real" Therian,
- a hierarchy of value,
- a clinical severity scale,
- or an automatic indicator of need for therapy.

Potential use:
individualized description and support,
especially where high animality creates friction with human social norms.

Status:
AUTHOR-DEVELOPED FRAMEWORK — NOT STANDARD COMMUNITY TERMINOLOGY


### Historical / Community Claims Requiring Verification

- Early definitions of Therian / therianthrope.
- Whether involuntariness has consistently been considered central.
- Historical treatment of voluntary identities.
- Development of Otherhearted, Copinglink, Otherlink, and Otherpaw.
- Historical use of "true Therian" and related gatekeeping debates.
- Relationship between online role-play trends and Therian terminology.
- Whether "Depth" or comparable concepts existed under other names.


### Empirical Claims Requiring Verification

- Whether Therian identity can properly be described as innate.
- Age of onset and developmental course.
- Whether species dysphoria commonly decreases with age.
- Relationship between acceptance and reduction of species dysphoria.
- Whether animality intensity predicts social friction or support needs.


### Current Canonical Candidate

A Therian is a person who experiences a persistent and personally
significant identification as, or as partly, a non-human animal.

This identity is generally described within Therian communities
as non-voluntary and distinct from role-play, performance,
animal-themed hobbies, or identities deliberately adopted for
a specific purpose.

The way this identity is experienced varies considerably between
individuals. Visible animal behavior, shifting, species dysphoria,
or any particular level of "animality" should not be treated
as mandatory evidence of Therian identity.

People may revise the labels they use as their self-understanding develops.


### Boundary Principle: Identity vs Conduct

Project Undertaker should distinguish between
identity gatekeeping and conduct boundaries.

An external observer should be cautious about declaring
that another person's internal identity is false
based on limited behavior, appearance, age, community affiliation,
or isolated mistakes.

However, uncertainty about another person's identity
does not require unconditional acceptance of harmful conduct.

Behavior that materially harms others,
violates consent,
deliberately exploits community trust,
or damages the Personhood of others
may be restricted or rejected regardless of the actor's identity label.

In short:

Do not certify or invalidate another person's inner identity lightly.

Do set boundaries around harmful behavior.

Community safety and identity authenticity are separate questions.

# Community Knowledge Transmission and Access Audit

## Purpose

This section records observed problems in the transmission,
accessibility, interpretation, and preservation
of Therianthropy-related knowledge.

These observations should not be treated as definitions
of Therian identity itself.

They are relevant to Project Undertaker,
Project DeepDivers,
reference-service design,
and future Access Provision.


## Author Field Observation: Reference-Service Gap

SheilaGrace reports that, during attempts to provide information
and reference support to Japanese-speaking people interested
in Therianthropy, a recurring complaint was that commonly recommended
resources provided broad definitions but insufficient practical detail.

The author's informal contact population was approximately 50 individuals.

Roughly one third of those individuals,
in the author's recollection,
expressed some form of dissatisfaction with the depth,
accessibility, or practical usefulness of existing reference material.

This was not a randomized survey
and should not be treated as a population estimate.

Status:

AUTHOR FIELD OBSERVATION — ANECDOTAL / CONVENIENCE SAMPLE


## Reference-Service Design Implication

Neutral definitions alone may be insufficient
for people attempting to understand unfamiliar identity concepts.

Future reference material should therefore consider providing:

- neutral definitions,
- explicit statements of what a concept does not mean,
- multiple contrasting examples,
- first-person experience examples,
- self-exploration questions,
- historical and community context,
- and routes to deeper source material.

Examples must be clearly labeled as examples
rather than universal models.


## Informal Mentorship and Oral Transmission

SheilaGrace reports that experienced community members,
including people described as Graymuzzles,
often functioned informally as mentors.

Their role was not merely to repeat dictionary definitions.

They provided concrete examples,
personal experience,
historical context,
and practical interpretation
that helped newer community members understand
how abstract terminology related to lived experience.

This should currently be described as
informal mentorship and oral / experiential transmission,
not as a formally established community-wide Mentor system.


## Platform, Language, and Filter-Bubble Risks

SheilaGrace observed several barriers
between interested individuals and deeper reference material:

- language barriers,
- reluctance to leave familiar social platforms,
- preference for short-form content,
- dependence on familiar interfaces,
- limited willingness to consult long-form sources,
- platform-specific filter bubbles,
- and risk of misinformation being reproduced
  through socially influential but poorly informed sources.

The author also reports cases in which
long-form written material received little direct attention,
while derivative short-form social-media reproductions
received substantially greater exposure.

These observations require external verification
before being generalized beyond the author's experience.


## Generational Transmission Concern

SheilaGrace experienced a decline
in the availability of experienced community members
willing to provide detailed mentorship and historical context.

The author associates this partly with
community fatigue,
platform changes,
generational separation,
and conflict surrounding newer social-media cultures.

The specific causal role of TikTok
or any other platform
has not yet been independently established.

Status:

AUTHOR FIELD OBSERVATION / HYPOTHESIS — VERIFICATION REQUIRED


## Project Undertaker / Project DeepDivers Implication

Preservation of information is insufficient
if future individuals cannot locate,
understand,
or practically use that information.

Material Preservation should therefore be paired with:

- Access Provision,
- interpretive scaffolding,
- source provenance,
- examples,
- translation where feasible,
- and pathways from introductory material
  to deeper reference sources.

The purpose is not to replace personal exploration
or community interaction.

The purpose is to reduce preventable information loss,
misinterpretation,
and dependence on a single platform,
mentor,
community,
or generation.


## Claims Requiring External Verification

- Historical role of Graymuzzles in community mentoring.
- Historical continuity between AHWW and later communities.
- Changes in experienced-member participation over time.
- Effects of TikTok and other short-form platforms
  on Therian terminology and public representation.
- Current accessibility and depth of major Therian reference resources.
- Development of Japanese-language Therian communities.
- Current state of Japanese-language Therian reference services.
